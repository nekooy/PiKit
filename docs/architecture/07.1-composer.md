# The composer, and the bottom of the screen

*[Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) §7.1.*

`enableEdgeToEdge()` sets `decorFitsSystemWindows = false`: the window is full-screen,
the IME is drawn *over* the app rather than resizing it, and the app is responsible for
keeping its own content out from under both. Measured on the emulator: the activity
window's frame stays `[0,0][1080,2400]` while the IME window is `[0,63][1080,2400]`
with `mImeHeight = 820`.

## What was tried, and why each one failed

1. **`AnimatedVisibility` driven by `WindowInsets.isImeVisible`.** That flag
   flips several times while one keyboard animation plays, as the IME's insets
   settle, so the bar expanded and collapsed repeatedly and every flip re-measured
   the whole page above it.
2. **`if (!imeVisible)` on top of the padding that already moved the bar.** Two
   mechanisms deciding the same thing: the bar appeared while the page resized in
   the opposite direction.
3. **The bar inside the keyboard's inset**, so it rode *up* and sat on top of the
   keyboard rather than getting out of the way. It shipped twice because it
   *looked* plausible: the bar really did move, just in the wrong direction.

## What is there now

The strip is pinned to the window's own bottom edge, outside the keyboard's inset,
and the **page**, not the strip, is padded by the keyboard: the keyboard rises over
the strip and hides it, and the page above ends exactly at the keyboard's top edge.

```
page bottom inset = max(navigationBars, ime, tabStripHeight)
tab strip         = aligned to the window bottom, inset = navigationBars only
```

There is no visibility state, no animation and no second clock: the strip's
disappearance *is* the keyboard's own motion, so the two cannot disagree. Swiping the
keyboard down reveals the strip progressively instead of snapping it back.

The strip is Miuix's `NavigationBar` now, and its item is a **fixed height** with the
label centred inside it: a label that does not fit is cut by the item rather than growing
it. That is the failure the bar this replaced had, and it is why the strip's height is
still *measured* rather than computed. The old item measured its label with loose
constraints, so a label wider than the item **wrapped**: measured on the emulator in
Japanese at font scale 1.8, `ターミナル` took two lines and grew its node to 158px against
82px for the three beside it, which pushed the strip's contents out of its own band — the
icons ended up drawn over the page above, and the page's inset is the strip's *measured*
height, so the thing it made room for was the wrong thing. §9.1 has the strip itself.

## The trap: `safeDrawing` already contains `ime`

`safeDrawing` is `systemBars + displayCutout + ime`. Using it as the strip's own
inset therefore added the keyboard's height to the strip's position and pushed it up
on top of the keyboard — design 3 again. Measured with `uiautomator`: the strip's
labels landed at `y = 1454` with the keyboard up, against `y = 2274` with it down — a
delta of 826 px, and the keyboard reported `mImeHeight = 820`. A missing inset and a
wrong inset look identical in a screenshot; they do not look identical in a number.
The fix is `WindowInsets.navigationBars`. The strip's height is measured with
`onSizeChanged` and fed back into the page's padding rather than hard-coded, because
the bar's own band is a Material figure this app does not own: it measures 231 px on
the 1080 px emulator, 168 px of it the bar's items and 63 px the gesture strip below
them, and the page above is padded by that measured total.

## A text field that looked full width and was not

The composer is a full-bleed band with three chips on a line above the field, the framed field
under them, and the field's own action row on its last line. **Since the Miuix rewrite the
field is the library's `TextField`**, which owns its whole box — fill, corner, placeholder,
focused border — so the composer no longer wraps a bare field in a frame of its own (§13); what
it does not own is the trap below, which is about `BasicTextField` inside it and has not moved.

A `Box` with `weight(1f)` around a `BasicTextField(modifier = Modifier.fillMaxWidth())` fails
on the device: the field measured **312 px wide inside a 780 px pill** — exactly the width of the
placeholder text, so text wrapped there — and everything to the right of that was inert, so
tapping the composer usually did nothing. `BasicTextField` measures its `decorationBox` with
`minWidth = 0` and reports *that* size, so a `fillMaxWidth` chained to it is undone one node
later. The fixed width a parent gives a **weighted child** arrives as a *minimum* and
survives, so `weight(1f)` belongs on the field itself, with no `Box` in between — and the
composer's field needs no weight at all, because it is a child of the band's own column and
a `Column` offers each child the full width as a minimum rather than as a weight. It is the number to check the next time this is doubted: on
the same 1080 px emulator the field's own box spans the band's inner width rather than the
width of its own placeholder.

## The composer's control row

The state, the model and the thinking level used to be three read-only rows of the
chat header, which put the three things a user changes most often while talking as
far from the message they apply to as the page allows. They are controls now, on a
row **above** the input box, each opening a dropdown: thinking level, model, and the
prompt cache's hit rate. Above, not below: a chip between the field and the keyboard
sits in the busiest strip on a phone screen and reads as part of the message being
written, where a chip above the field reads as the setting the next message will be
sent with. The row is a `LazyRow` so three chips whose values can each be long stay on
one line by scrolling rather than by being squeezed. A plain `Row` with
`horizontalScroll` under `fillMaxWidth` does not do this: it measures its children
against the screen's width and then draws the overflow past the edge, so the last chip
is sliced by the window — measured, `缓存命中 0%` rendered at x=966..1048 and was cut
rather than scrolled to.

**The row used to hold four chips, and the context share was one of them.** That share
is the ring beside the send now, on the field's own action row — because the chip row is a
row of *choices*, the level and the model the next message will be sent with, and
`上下文 12%` is a reading. Two readings of one report took half of a four-chip row that
already had to scroll, and a *share* is the one that a shape can carry: a 20dp ring
filled a third of the way round says what the pill said and leaves the row to the two
values that are genuinely picked. `ContextRing` draws it — track in `outline`, fill in
`primary`, the fill turning to the error role at 90% — and a tap opens the same
fourteen-row dropdown the cache chip does, so the numbers are still one gesture away.
Its touch target is 36dp, and the sentence the pill carried as its label is the ring's
`contentDescription` instead.

The three chips are a Miuix `Surface` on `surfaceContainerHigh`, a full-round clip, a 14dp
glyph and the label — the app's own chip shape, at one place (§13). The label is drawn in
**`onSurface`**, not the muted `onSurfaceContainerHigh`: each chip carries the *current value*
of a setting (`medium`, `deepseek-v4`, `缓存命中 0%`), and #A2A2A2 on the #E8E8E8 fill was a
2.5:1 pair — below the 3:1 floor for anything a reader must read, and the reason the chips
were read as faded rather than as controls. Each passes no `contentDescription`, because the
chip's own label is what the control says and the label is the value the reader wants rather
than the noun they can already infer from where the chip sits.

**The chips and the ring open dropdown menus, upward, in the library's own row.** The thinking
and model chips always did; the cache chip opened a bottom sheet of fourteen two-line rows — a
page-sized panel for a question the reader asks by glancing at a number — and that sheet is a
dropdown now, for the same reason every other menu on this page is one (§7.1's own rule): a
menu that answers one question belongs at the control that asks it. They open through
`WindowListPopup` with `aboveAnchorPositionProvider()`, which pins the popup's bottom edge to
the anchor's top: the library's own provider picks by fit, and a chip row pinned above the
input field has no room below it — the fit test put the menu over the field, over the send
button, and once the keyboard was up off the visible window. The thinking-level and model
menus' rows are the library's `DropdownImpl` — title over summary, the tick at
`CheckIconSize` in `primary`, the selected title also `primary` — and the model menu was the
last one drawing its own row (a `PiMenuRow` with a hand-placed `Ok` glyph), which is what
made two menus over the same conversation mark their current answer two different ways.
The context menu — the report the cache chip and the ring both open — is the one menu here
with no choices in it at all, and its rows say so: `PiMenuRow` with no `onClick`, which keeps
the library's title-over-summary metrics and the scheme's normal ink while reporting no role
and consuming no tap. They were `DropdownImpl(isSelected = false, enabled = false)` for a
while — "a read-only fact is not a radio, so switch the row off" — and what the disabled ink
says to a reader is that fourteen numbers the app has just measured are fourteen things they
are not allowed to pick. *Disabled* is a state of a control, and there is no control here.
The model menu's summary reads `name/provider`, with a missing key on a line of its own: the
middot-joined run-on it used to be is a shape nothing else in the app names a configuration
with, and the model settings page writes the same pair the same way (§9.2).

**The chip's fill is the palette's step for a control, and it took four answers to get
there.** A chip the *same colour* as the composer it sits on read as four labels with glyphs
beside them rather than as four controls, and a selected state was the only thing on it that
looked pressable. A `primaryContainer` iteration followed — a step away from the band and
visibly *coloured*, with the label on `onPrimaryContainer` — and so did a neutral step up
(`surfaceContainerHighest`) that bought a shade where the question had been colour, and a 1dp
hairline around that step that drew a box around a fill which was already a boundary (§13).
The chip the rewrite inherited, and the one this page draws, is the palette's own step with
no arithmetic on top: a Miuix `Surface` on `surfaceContainerHigh`, the label in `onSurface`.
The luminance test the hand-rolled chip before it carried is not the same device and was not
reinstated: that one asked the *palette* how far apart two steps were, and a palette is not
a question a call site should be able to ask.

**The action row's two glyphs are sized by their ink, not by their boxes.** ⌘ and `+` are the
same 40dp button with the same tint, and they were two visibly different marks: Miuix's `Add`
draws its ink out to 84% of its box (measured from the vector — the paths span 95→1062 of the
1152-unit viewport) where Lucide's `command`, drawn to 21 of its 24 units, is nearly all ink.
At the 23dp the plus used it was 19dp of mark against the ⌘'s 15dp. Both are 17dp now — 14.3dp
of ink and 14.1dp — and the plus is a *smaller box* than it was, which is the counter-intuitive
half: for two glyphs from different sets, equalising the drawn ink is what makes them read as
one row.

## The band, and the field's own box

The composer is the page's bottom band: a full-bleed `Surface` on **`surface`** — the same
colour the tab strip and the root scaffold behind it are painted in — in the page's column,
under the transcript rather than over it. It was `surfaceContainer`, which made the band a
white box under a grey bar and left a horizontal seam where the two met; on `surface` the
foot of the page is one surface from the transcript's end to the navigation bar. The notices
sit in the same column, above the band. The list's own box therefore ends where the band
begins, its tail padding is one line of `bodyMedium`, and the jump-to-latest button needs no
clearance from anything: a message scrolls *to* the band rather than behind it, and the
measured height the overlay used to keep is gone. There is no shadow and no hairline: the
composer's own white frame against this grey *is* the boundary, and a drawn rule here would
compete with the transcript's dividers.

The field has a **box of its own**, and the box is a Miuix `Surface` — `surfaceContainer`
(white) at the kit's corner ([§13](13-design-language.md)) — that *contains* the field and
the row's four controls: the command list and the attach button at its left corner, and the
context ring and the send disc at its right. The controls sat in a row under the field for
one release, on the argument that a control between the field and the keyboard is in the
busiest strip on the screen; what that produced was a field with no visible box of its own
(the band was the box), a send button as far from the text it sends as the band is wide, and
a row that read as page furniture rather than as part of the composer. Inside the frame the
field itself paints
nothing — `piFieldColors(background = Color.Transparent)`, so the library's focus border
animates to nothing rather than to a grey rectangle — and the frame is the only outline on
screen. The box the rewrite first drew was the library's own `secondaryContainer` fill with a
2dp `primary` focus border; the border is off everywhere in the app for the reason §13
records, and the ring's disappearance is what made a frame the field's own necessary again.
The panel version had no frame at all: the panel's own rounded outline said where the field
was, so a second box inside it would have been a box inside a box, and that argument holds
*while the composer floats*. A band has no outline, so the field needed its own — the report
is "输入框形状改回 0.4.1 的样子，取代现在的悬浮样式" — and the frame is what makes the band
read as a strip that contains a place to write rather than as an area of the page.

The **send control is a disc**, and the one fully round control on the page: the accent pair
while it can send, the error role with a filled square while an answer is streaming.
`IconButton`'s own corner is 40dp on a 40dp button — already a circle — and the radius is
stated as 20dp explicitly so the shape does not depend on those two constants staying equal.

**The two discs' fills are stated against the frame, not against the palette's ramp.** The
idle send disc used to be `surfaceContainerHigh` on the composer's `surfaceContainer` frame —
two roles the dark scheme gives the *same* #242424, so the disc disappeared and the arrow
floated in the corner on its own; the stop disc was `errorContainer`, which in the light scheme
is #FDF6F4 (a disc the colour of paper) and in the dark one #2E0603 against a #242424 frame.
The idle disc is `PiTone.inset()` — the wash that is a step from whatever it is drawn on, in
both schemes — and the stop disc is `PiTone.error()` with the scheme's `onError` square. The
same correction is why the tool output panel, the reasoning panel and the status pill are
washes rather than `surfaceContainerHigh`: the dark ramp is flat between `surfaceContainer` and
`surfaceContainerHigh`, and anything that relied on that step had no fill at all in the dark
scheme (§13).

**The marks are an arrow and a square, not a paper plane and a pause.** Every chat app this
page is read beside sends with an arrow and stops with a square; the plane the library's `Send`
draws is a *postal* figure that says "share" in most Android surfaces, and `Pause` is the media
figure — two bars meaning "pause playback", which an answer is not. Both vectors are borrowed
(`ic_arrow_up.xml`, `ic_stop.xml`) and both are sized at the call site: 22dp of box for the
arrow's 14-of-24 ink, 24dp for the square's 12.

**The band leaves 10dp above the keyboard.** The root's `imePadding` ends the page exactly at
the keyboard's top edge, so the frame — the last object in the page's column — was drawn
against the keyboard with both of its bottom corners on that edge, and a field that is part of
a keyboard looks like one. The gap is read in the composer, where it is drawn, from the two
insets the page's own padding is made of: `max(ime, navigationBars)` is the page's bottom edge,
so what is left of the keyboard is the distance from its edge to the navigation bar. Ten dp
while that is more than the gap; eased to nothing over the last four gaps of the edge's travel,
so with no keyboard the band still meets the tab strip exactly.

**Both of those are corrections, and each one has a measurement.** The gap used to be computed
above the scaffold — where the ime inset is still unconsumed, because `imePadding` hands its
children zero — and handed down as a composition local. A local whose value moves flips its
*content*, so the chat page recomposed before the gap did: a screen recording of one close showed
the composer travelling down with the keyboard for four frames, holding for five (about 80 ms)
**26 px — 10dp at this density — above its resting place**, then hopping that last step once the
page had recomposed. Moving the read down to the composer was half of the fix. The other half is
which *phase* reads it: in composition, every inset the platform reported during the animation
recomposed the whole card — the field, the chip row, four buttons and the send disc — and the height
the layout used was the last composition's. Recorded again with that read still in composition, the
band was **a full 10dp above its rest on the last frame the keyboard was visible**, and stayed there
for the recorder's next frame. It is a `Spacer(Modifier.layout { … WindowInsets.ime.getBottom(…) })`
now — a layout-phase read, exactly what `imePadding` does at the root — so it is placed on every frame
the inset moves and nothing recomposes: the same close puts the band within 3dp of its rest on the
frame the keyboard disappears and at rest one frame later.

The switch at the end of the travel was wrong for a second reason: the platform's inset animation
is coarse. One close reported `ime` 844 → 282 → 219 → 125 → 94 → 32 → 10 → 5 → 1 → 0, 20–30 ms
apart, so a gap that stayed 10dp until the last 10dp of keyboard and then went to zero crossed its
whole range in a single frame. A ramp four gaps long spreads that distance over four or five
frames, and the only thing it changes while the keyboard is open is the last 40dp of the edge's
travel, where neither the keyboard nor the frame is being looked at. Recorded again after the
change, the same close runs 1406 → 1445 → 1916 → 2058 → 2068 → 2083 → 2084, with no frame holding
and no step.

What the panel bought, and what is given up with it, is worth naming, because it was a
deliberate design for a release. The panel floated: `PiShapes.panel` (28dp) on
`surfaceContainer`, inset 12dp on every side, with a 12dp **ambient-only** shadow
(`spotColor = Color.Transparent`, because a platform shadow's spot half is cast from above
and to one side and gave a full-width strip a dark band under one edge and none on the
other). That said "this is in front of the list" without a drawn line, and it let a message
scroll *past* the composer. What it could not say is where the field itself was, and the
reader's answer was that they wanted the field framed. A band in the column gives the same
separation by position — nothing overlaps, so nothing has to be told apart — and the fill
step does the rest.

The agent's **state** is deliberately not one of the chips: it is a fact rather than
a control, and a row of tappable chips with one inert chip at its head makes the eye
check all four every time. It is `PiPage`'s subtitle — one line under the title, drawn by
the kit and ellipsised by it, because the library's own bar renders a subtitle with no line
limit and a sentence wide enough to wrap would grow the bar and every page's content
rectangle with it ([§7.3](07.3-transcript.md)). The agent's mark that used to sit beside it
is gone. Nothing in the composer carries the *live* tone either: live means a turn is
running, and a control the reader is about to type into is not — the stop button, which
*is* the "something is running" control, is the `errorContainer` one, and live is the
palette's secondary pair everywhere it is drawn (`PiTone.live`, [§13](13-design-language.md)).

What the state used to be drawn beside is worth keeping, because it is the reason a
mark is not simply placed next to the subtitle text: the coloured dot was an
`InlineTextContent` placeholder *inside* the text rather than a `Box` beside it,
since a `Row` of a `Box` and a `Text` gives the box no baseline, and at bodySmall the
5dp dot sat 3px low. The dot is gone with the flat header; a `Box` beside a line of
text is still the thing that measurement rules out.

The cache chip is measured against the **last finalized message**, not the session
total, because prompt caching is a property of one request: a ratio accumulated
over a session reports the average of a conversation that has changed shape several
times. It reads `0%` rather than hiding when nothing has been cached, because the
control people reach for would then disappear exactly when they go looking for it.

**The chip's fill is one step per palette, and the steps are not the same size, and
the design system's chip is what makes that the palette's question rather than the
call site's.** The row went 26dp-with-a-hairline to 34dp-with-`surfaceContainerHigh`
to `surfaceVariant`, and the last of those was still hard to see in the
light theme: on the composer's `#EDEEF2` bar, `#E4E9EF` is a 9/5/3-per-channel
difference, which at 34dp reads as a printed label rather than as a button.
`composerChipContainer()` took one more step — `surfaceContainer`, `#DCE3EB`,
another 8/6/4 down — and *no* step in the dark theme, where the same move would put
the chip at `#1A2028` on a `#171C23` bar and erase it, and the choice was made from
the fill's luminance, because the colour is the question being asked. That is a
luminance test at a call site to answer a question the palette should answer, and it
is what the design language means by a page not inventing a colour: the hand-rolled
chip and `composerChipContainer()` are both gone, and `PiSelectChip` is a 32dp pill
on `surfaceContainerHigh`, which against this well (`surfaceContainerLow`) is two
steps in **both** schemes — visible without asking the colour anything. Measured on
the emulator, the drawn pill is 84 px (32dp) inside its 48dp touch target.

The bar's two actions are ordered **history, new**: left-to-right as "look at the
others, start another", and also the order of how much each disturbs what is on
screen — the session list is a page away, and a new session replaces the transcript.
They are `PiPage`'s `actions` — Miuix `IconButton`s in the bar's action row, drawn with the
library's `Recent` and `Add` ([§7.3](07.3-transcript.md)) — and there is nothing
beside them since the agent's mark went.

A third action, **compress**, used to stand first here: `session.compact()` under the
library's `Merge`, ordered as "do something to this conversation, look at the others,
start another" with a compaction applied in place. It is gone, because the composer's ⌘
list already carries `/compact`: the same operation twice on one page, and the bar's copy
was an icon on a row of *navigation* that opened nothing — a reader looking for it has
one place to go, which is the page's own list of one-shot operations on the conversation.

The row under the field holds four controls, and its order is "what goes into the
message" and then "what happens to it": the command list and the `+` together at the
**left** edge, a weighted spacer, then the context ring and the send (or stop) at the
right. The two adds are the same kind of control — one plain 40dp icon button each, one
list of things to put into the message or run against it — so they sit beside each
other at the edge a sentence starts from, and the ring, which is a *reading* rather than
an add, sits beside the action that consumes it. That order replaced ` ⌘ … ◔ + → `,
whose argument was adjacency rather than kind: the `+` was beside the send because that
is the half of the row it had been added to, and the ring was at the `+`'s left because
its menu is one the cache chip opens too. What it produced was a row whose two "add"
buttons were as far apart as the row is wide, with the two controls that are *not* adds
between them — an order grouped by where a control had been added rather than by what it
does. The spacer between the halves is what keeps the layout independent of how wide the
text is. The row is on the field's own last line rather than under the frame, because the
frame is the field's box and a control below it would sit between the text and the
keyboard.
Send and stop are Miuix `IconButton`s at a drawn 40dp (measured 105 px on the emulator),
whose own default *is* a fully round pill at that minimum, so nothing here states a radius:
the two states are the same shape on purpose, because the reader is looking for one button
there, not two. The stop state is the one place in the
composer that is not the app's own voice — the error role, because stopping is the action
that cannot be taken back, and because a disc that says "not yet" and one that says "now
stop" have to be told apart by colour as well as by glyph. The ring just before it is the
context meter, and it is *not* a button of the same kind: 36dp of touch target around a
20dp arc, with no fill of its own and no label.

## Why the model switch is not a restart

Switching profile used to mean "make it active, then `scheduleRestart()`": a process
teardown, a `pi` start and a fresh handshake — seconds, and the whole transcript
re-read — to change one field that pi accepts over RPC through `set_model`. So the
switch is applied over RPC and the restart is only the fallback for when pi refuses,
which also means the running turn, the queue and the terminal's view of the session
survive it. Two details are load-bearing: the provider is sent as **pi's own provider
id** (`deepseek`, `pikit-custom`), because `set_model` matches `m.provider ===
command.provider` against the snapshot of models pi already knows — checked in pi
0.85.1's `rpc-mode.js`, which answers `Model not found: <provider>/<id>` for anything
else; the environment variable name is the same string for the built-in providers but
not the same concept, and sending `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` is a lookup that always misses.
And the profile is committed to the store *first*, so the setting survives a later
restart even if the call is rejected — a rejection means this build does not offer
that model, and the fallback is a restart, which cannot be wrong.

## Why there is no segmented control in this app

A `SegmentedChoice` — one track, one marker sliding under the chosen segment —
lived in `Forms.kt` for a while, used for exactly one setting: the thinking level,
on the model page. It was removed rather than fixed for two reasons:

- **Seven Han labels do not fit a phone's width.** The control's own documentation
  said two to five options; the thinking levels are seven, so each segment got
  about 36dp and the touch targets were under the platform minimum.
- **It rendered as an empty box.** The control sized itself from its *first
  segment*: `BoxWithConstraints` wraps its content and the segments are sized from
  its marker, so seven options produced a ~150px rounded rectangle at the start of
  the row and every label ellipsised to nothing. Measured with `uiautomator`: not
  one text node inside the control's bounds, against seven rows in the sheet that
  replaced it.

The design system shipped a connected group of options (`PiConnectedGroup`, §13) for
a while — the guidance's own shape for a short set of alternatives shown side by side —
and it never sent the thinking level back to a segmented control: a segment that
does not render is a defect a better component fixes, but the first failure is about
how many options there are, and seven labels that do not fit a phone's width do not
fit it in a new component either. No page had a use for it in the end, and it was
deleted with its last call site.

**The composer's chip is the only control for it, and it opens a floating menu.** A
thinking level is a *range* from `off` to `max`, and the first answer to the chip was
a discrete slider (`ThinkingLevelSheet`, the same shape as the personalization page's
font-size sheet): the level under the thumb as the big name, its sentence underneath,
ticks on a track whose ends are pi's first and last ids, the drag a draft that
committed on release — applying mid-drag re-measured the composer's chip row under the
finger and made the drag stutter. The slider is gone, and the menu at the chip is the
shape every other choice in the app now wears: a `WindowListPopup` anchored to the chip,
its rows `DropdownImpl`s from Miuix's popup list, with the tick on the current level. The
ids alone, no sentences under them — seven two-line entries out of seven one-word choices
was the report `思考等级那里去掉每个等级下面的小字说明`. The sentences are gone from the
app now, not moved: the level is pi's wire string, and the model page reports it as a fact
on each model's own row (`thinkingLevelFor`) rather than explaining what an id means. What
the slider knew and the menu keeps: the seven ids are never translated, and a model with
one level gets a menu of one row where the slider drew a one-tick track. The model
page used to carry the setting as a `PickerRow` at the top of **Settings → Model &
provider**, and that row is gone: one setting had grown two controls, and the composer's
chip is the one a conversation actually uses. The page lists profiles, and the level
appears on it only as a *fact* — the level each model would run at is on that model's own
row, clamped by `clampThinkingLevel` against the levels pi last reported for it — which is
what makes one helper enough for a chip, a menu and a row.

## Sheets are drawn in the page's own window, not in a second one

Every modal in the app — the pickers, the conversation's numbers, the command list,
the file preview, the terminal's session switcher — used to be a Material3
`ModalBottomSheet`, which is not a view in the page but a `ComponentDialog`
(`ModalBottomSheetDialogWrapper`) laid over the activity. Neither of the two things
wrong with it could be fixed from the call sites, because a dialog window is defined
to take window focus.

**It closed the keyboard.** A focusable window appearing over a focused text field
takes the IME's connection with it. Measured: `mImeWindowVis=3 mInputShown=true`
before tapping a composer chip, `mImeWindowVis=0 mInputShown=false` 250 ms after.
Three animations then ran at once and disagreed — the keyboard leaving, the page
re-measuring as the IME's inset collapsed (the composer's chips moved 610 px, from
y=1153 to y=1763), and the sheet travelling in against an inset that was still
shrinking under it, so it arrived somewhere other than where it settled.

**It swallowed input after closing.** The window outlives the state that asked for
it. Counting `pi.kit.mob` windows with `dumpsys window` after a dismiss-then-tap
pair at increasing gaps: one window while open, and the tap after the dismissal was
lost at gaps of 150, 250 and 500 ms and only landed at 800 ms. Roughly 0.6–0.9 s of
dead screen, per sheet, everywhere a sheet was used.

A sheet is now a `Box` in the page's own window (`ui/components/Sheets.kt`), drawn
by the root above the tab strip. There is no second window, so there is nothing to
take focus, nothing to re-measure and nothing to outlive the state. Two
consequences are deliberate:

- **The keyboard stays up**, and the panel is lifted above it by
  `WindowInsets.ime`, so a picker opened mid-sentence appears over the keyboard
  rather than replacing it. The page underneath does not move at all.
- **A leaving sheet takes no input.** The panel's `draggable` and every row's
  `clickable` are installed only while the host says the sheet is open, so once it
  has been dismissed a tap reaches whatever is underneath — the same measurement
  now reads: lost at a 0.00–0.10 s gap, landed from 0.15 s on, which is the 250 ms
  of its own exit animation and nothing more. A pointer modifier that is merely
  *disabled* is not enough: it still claims the hit test, so a row on its way off
  screen would eat the tap instead of letting it through.

`SheetHost` holds one sheet — modal means never two — and keeps the last one for the
length of the exit, which is what the dialog's window used to do for free. The state
lives above the root's `key(language)` block: switching the language rebuilds every
screen under it, and the sheet it was switched *from* still has to finish sliding
out. A sheet's body is a composable rather than a row spec because the file preview
is not a list, and a body that must follow live data reads it inside the lambda,
since the lambda is built once, when the sheet opens. Only the *content* of a sheet
is drawn by the sheet: the panel, its shape, the grabber, the scrim, the back gesture
and the drag all belong to the layer, so the picker and the command list cannot
drift apart. The *bodies* are the design system's now — `PiSheetTitle`,
`PiSheetList`, `PiSheetRow`, `PiSheetActions` (§13) — so every sheet in the app is drawn
from the same parts and none of them can drift from another either.

**A modifier's `graphicsLayer` alpha reaches only the modifiers after it in the chain, and
the scrim is where getting that wrong ships as a black page.** The catcher was
`background(scrim).graphicsLayer { alpha = … }`: a modifier wraps the ones after it, so
the fill painted *outside* the layer at full strength whatever the alpha said, and every
menu and sheet opened over an opaque black page in both themes
(`点击按钮弹出悬浮菜单后，后面的内容全部黑屏了`). The order is
`graphicsLayer { alpha = … }.background(scrim)`. The layer's older version of the same
code was fine for a reason that is easy to lose: the colour itself carried the alpha
(`scrim.copy(alpha = …)`), so there was no layer for the order to be wrong in. The rule
generalises — an alpha that governs a paint belongs in a layer ahead of that paint, whose
lambda is read at draw time.

**`Sheet`'s `content` is the last constructor parameter, and that is load-bearing.** Every
call site passes it as a trailing lambda, so a parameter added after it breaks all of them
at once; the optionals (`anchor`, `alignEnd`, `fullBleedBottom`, `preferAbove`) come first.

**The conversation list's row menu was the last `DropdownMenu` in the app.** `Popup`
emits a zero-size layout node into its caller's layout, and the row was a `Row` with
`Arrangement.spacedBy(4.dp)` — which does not skip zero-size children — so merely
opening the menu slid the ⋮ button 4 dp to the left and narrowed the title column by
the same amount. And a `DropdownMenu` is anchored to the composable that contains
it, so it opened under the *row's* left edge rather than under the button, over the
title it belonged to. It became a sheet body first, and it is a *floating menu* now —
which is the anchored half of this same layer, and the answer to what a sheet does
with a question as small as one row's three actions.

## The anchored half: a menu at the thing it is about

The composer's menus are Miuix's `OverlayListPopup` — the library's floating list, drawn in the
root `Scaffold`'s popup host — anchored at the control that opened it, one menu per control,
dismissed by a tap anywhere outside it. A trigger composes its popup beside itself, so the two share
a parent and the library can place the panel against the button rather than against the page.
The rows are `PiMenuRow`, which copies `DropdownImpl`'s popup-mode metrics — `body1` at medium
weight for the title, `body2` for the summary, 20dp at the sides, 56dp of height — so a list
of them sits beside a list of the library's own rows without a seam. Every row dismisses the
panel *before* it acts, so a second tap aimed at the page behind a menu that is leaving cannot
land on one of its rows. `ListPopupColumn` is the scrolling body: `MENU_MAX_HEIGHT` bounds it
and the list scrolls inside that bound.

**The overlay popup rather than the window one, and the keyboard is why.** `WindowListPopup` is a
`Dialog`, and a focusable window appearing over a focused text field takes the IME's connection with
it — the same mechanism that took the sheets out of dialog windows (§7.1, *Sheets are drawn in the
page's own window*), and here it cost the reader the keyboard every time they reached for a chip:
`mImeWindowVis=3 mInputShown=true` before the tap, `mImeWindowVis=0 mInputShown=false` after it, and
the keyboard stayed down once the menu was dismissed. `OverlayListPopup` is the same panel rendered
by `MiuixPopupUtils` inside the page's own window, so nothing takes the focus: the same tap now
leaves `mInputShown=true` with the caret still in the field, measured on the emulator with the menu
open. It also removes the second window's other habit — outliving the page that raised it — which
the roster in the terminal tab had the same reason to want gone.

**The composer's four menus always open above their trigger.** The chips and buttons that ask
for a menu sit above the field the reader is typing into, and a panel that drops below them
lands on that field — the library's own provider picks by fit, and the fit test at the bottom
of the window puts the panel in its "centred" branch, over the field it belongs to.
`aboveAnchorPositionProvider` is the replacement: the panel's bottom edge sits on the trigger's
top edge less its margin, and the result is clamped into the window, so a menu taller than the
space above slides down against its trigger rather than off the status bar. The arithmetic is
in `PiPopupPosition`.

**The shadow rides the fade, and the rest of this section is history.** The paragraphs that
follow were written against the anchored `Sheet` this page's menus used before Miuix 0.9.4's
`WindowListPopup` replaced it; the names they use (`SheetHost.progress`, the panel's own
`SubcomposeLayout`) went with that implementation. They are kept because each one settled a
rule that still holds — a shadow on the same clock as its panel, one measure pass, a dropdown's
own 125 ms — and a rule whose reasoning is deleted is a rule the next change re-learns by
breaking it. The first version hung the menu's shadow on the
`Surface`'s `shadowElevation`, and the platform composites an elevation shadow
*outside* the `graphicsLayer` alpha that fades the panel in — so the panel arrived
over 125 ms while its shadow was at full strength on the first frame, which is the
report "悬浮菜单出现时后面的阴影过于生硬，是突然出现的那种". The elevation is now set
inside the same `graphicsLayer` lambda that reads `progress`, scaled by it: no
shadow at 0, the menu's full shadow at 1, on the one clock.

**The panel is placed in one measure pass, and it answers at dropdown speed.** Two
reports — "点击到出现都不够迅速且不流畅丝滑" — had one cause each. *Not smooth*: the
first version measured the panel after composing it, so the opening frame was laid out
from a zero size and corrected itself the next frame — a visible jump mid-fade, on
every menu that flips above its trigger (all of the composer's chips). The panel is a
`SubcomposeLayout` now: content composed, measured and placed inside one layout pass,
so the first frame is the frame it stays on. *Not rapid*: the layer's one 250 ms clock
is the page transition's, and a dropdown that takes a quarter-second to appear at the
control that was tapped reads as a pause — an anchored menu runs on 125 ms, the
platform `DropdownMenu`'s own fade-and-scale. Both halves of the animation read
`SheetHost.progress` inside `graphicsLayer`, at draw time; reading it in composition
rebuilt the whole modal layer on every frame of every entrance and exit, which was the
rest of the stutter. The scale grows out of the corner that faces the trigger — the
top edge when the menu drops below it, the bottom edge when it flips above, and
horizontally the edge the panel is *aligned* by — so a menu whose panel hangs to the
left of its trigger (end-aligned, as a row's action menu is) still appears from under
the button instead of coming in from the screen's other side
(`按钮在右边，动画效果却是菜单从左边弹出来的`). The sheet's translation would carry a
menu up from the window's edge, straight through the control it opened from.

The rule for which surface is which: a *choice the panel hangs off* is a menu, a
*report or a form* is a sheet. In practice:

- **the composer's thinking-level and model chips** open menus at the chip — the two
  choices a sentence is sent with, read once and tapped once. The `/model` command
  row opens the same model menu at the same chip, which is why that one anchor is
  owned by `ChatScreen` rather than by the chip;
- **the composer's ⌘ and + buttons** open menus at the button — the command list
  (each row runs on the tap; the `!` row opens the shell-line *sheet*, because a
  line to type needs a field) and the attach choices (camera, image, file). Both
  were sheets and both are short menus of things to *do*. Every command row carries a
  one-line description under its label, the `!` row included: its label is a single
  punctuation mark, so the summary is the only place that row can say what it does —
  `shellCommandHint`, the same sentence the shell field it opens shows under its title;
- **the conversation list's row actions** (hold a row) open a menu on the row —
  rename, pin, delete;
- **the terminal's session switcher** opens a menu at its toolbar button — the
  question is "which terminal", asked over the terminal.

Everything else stays a docked sheet: the conversation's numbers is a report to
read, and the forms (the AGENTS.md editor, the model numbers, the avatar picker)
need the bottom edge for their buttons. Settings' language row is a **dialog** —
the one selection the reader asked to be a 弹窗 (`语言选择改为弹窗选择的形式`), and
the shape fits: a question, its three answers under it, a confirm and a cancel.

## A list scrolls to the panel's edge; the inset is inside the scroll

A sheet body used to be padded by the navigation bar's height at the panel level, and
that padding is what stood between a scrolling list's text and the window's bottom
edge: the text stopped at the padding, the padding showed the panel's own fill, and a
scrolling list looked like it had a bar of white under it for as long as it scrolled
(`滑动时文字不会直接触底而是会被底下一个白色条块遮挡`). The same band showed on the
fetched-model list and every other list on the layer, because the inset was applied
once per panel, above whatever scrolled.

A body that scrolls now declares it (`Sheet.fullBleedBottom`), the layer hands it the
inset through `LocalSheetBottomInset` instead of padding the panel, and `PiSheetList`
carries it as the list's own bottom content padding — the inset is then *part of the
scroll*: text travels the full height of the panel, and the last item still clears the
gesture bar when the list is scrolled to its end. A body with something after its list
(a footnote, an action row, a form's buttons) still owes the inset itself, below that
tail, which is what `PickerBody`'s and this file's forms' own paddings do.
