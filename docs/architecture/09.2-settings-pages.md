# 9.2. The settings pages, and the furniture of a page

*[Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) §9.2.*

What the pages below the tab strip are made of: the app bar every one of them wears, the rows and cards
they are built from, and the settings pages whose content needed an argument of its own — those two
are §9.3, because this file reached the size at which a chapter is split (§9.3 is the web-access
page and the maintenance page, and nothing about them changed in the move).

## The shape, since the Miuix rewrite

The interface is drawn from Miuix, and the vocabulary the rest of this chapter uses is the one in
`ui/kit` and `ui/settings`:

- **A page is `PiPage(title, subtitle, onBack)`** — Miuix's `Scaffold` with a pinned
  `SmallTopAppBar`, and a subtitle that `PiPage` itself draws (§13 has the reason).
- **A group is `PiSection(title)`** — a Miuix `SmallTitle` over a `Card`. The rows inside it
  touch: the library's preference rows have no hairline, and the card's own surface is what
  separates one group from the next.
- **A row is a preference component** — `BasicComponent` for a statement, `ArrowPreference` for a
  row that opens something, `SwitchPreference`, `RadioButtonPreference`, `SliderPreference`,
  `TextField` — with its value in the trailing `endActions` slot, drawn by `SettingsValue`.
- **A dialog is `PiConfirm`**, or `SettingsAlert` where the body is the installer's
  own output; **a sheet is `PiSheet`**, and a picker is `SettingsPickerSheet`; **a transient
  message is `LocalPiToast`**. Every dialog's answers are drawn by **`PiDialogAnswers`**, the
  kit's one button row — two filled `TextButton`s of equal weight, the destructive one told
  apart by its label's colour — so a confirmation looks like itself whichever page raised it;
  the transparent-container pair two dialogs used to pass is gone (§13).

Two things went with the old design system, and the paragraphs below were written against them.
Most rows no longer carry a *leading mark*: Miuix's rows do have a `startAction` slot, but
re-deciding a glyph per row for concepts the app's own set drew (a model profile, a relay
protocol, an API key) would have been a new visual vocabulary rather than a migration, so the
slot is spent only where a mark earns its place — the add row on the web-search page, the
leading icon of a picker. And a row no longer has a `…Divider` between it and the next one — the
library draws a group as a card whose rows touch, which is what makes the "between rows" rule
below a statement about *fields* now.

The measurements and the rejected designs are kept as they were written: they are why the shape
is what it is. Where a paragraph named a component the rewrite retired, it now says so.

## The header is one fixed strip

A settings page is a `PiPage` — one Miuix `SmallTopAppBar` over a scrolling body (§13) — and the
bar's own *height* is what this section is about.

The reason is a defect in the library's own flexible pair: their expanded height is **two
different tokens depending on whether a subtitle is present**
(`MediumFlexibleAppBarWithoutSubtitleExpandedHeight` against `…WithSubtitleExpandedHeight`,
and the same pair for the large bar), so the heights were never equal. The pinned medium bar
measured **136dp** below the status bar on the 1080 px emulator where it carried a subtitle,
the large collapsing one **152dp** before it gave any of it back, and a page that passed no
subtitle was shorter again — and the large bar was on three of the app's pages (the chat page,
the files listing and the manual's own document). The strip above the body therefore changed
height between destinations and, on a collapsing page, within one scroll.

**Since the Miuix rewrite** the strip is `Scaffold`'s: a pinned `SmallTopAppBar` with
`MiuixScrollBehavior`, the surface colour and the status-bar inset supplied by the scaffold, and
the title and back arrow drawn by the bar itself. One line each is the library's own behaviour,
so the rules below are now statements about *what goes in the line* rather than about a box the
app measures. The subtitle is the app's one addition: `PiPage` draws it under the bar, one line,
ellipsised, because the library renders a `subtitle` with no line limit and a sentence that does
not fit wraps — measured on the terminal page, its English idle sentence took a second line where
the Chinese one did not, and the bar, and therefore the PTY's rectangle, moved by a line when the
language changed. `null` is a page with no subtitle and `""` reserves the line while the value is
still being read, which is what keeps the files page's content rectangle still while a folder is
being listed.

The shell the rewrite retired wore one strip at a fixed height: 56dp of content under the status
bar, one `titleLarge` line and one `bodySmall` line, grown by `heightIn` at a large font scale
rather than clipped, with the status-bar inset consumed *inside* that strip's own `Surface`. The
inset had been consumed above the bar before that, which is why the clock sat on a different colour
from the title the moment content scrolled under: the bar filled with `surfaceContainer` while the
strip behind the clock stayed on the page's surface. One container was the answer at the time, and
the fill was driven by `overlappedFraction` — the library's own answer to how much of the bar has
content behind it — read inside a `derivedStateOf` so a scroll recomposed the strip only when the
answer flipped.

Two library details decided the last of it, and neither is visible from the app bar's API.
`pinnedScrollBehavior` returned a `PinnedScrollBehavior` whose `snapAnimationSpec` is **null**, so a
pinned bar had nothing to *restore* it after a fling and could be left permanently collapsed; the
shell set `barState.heightOffsetLimit` by hand for that reason, because a pinned behaviour never set
it and `overlappedFraction` divides by it. And `overlappedFraction` flips on `> 0.01` rather than on
any overlap at all, which is what made a pinned bar fill as soon as it scrolled instead of
flickering on the first pixel. None of it survives the rewrite: a Miuix bar keeps its own height, and
the app hands it nothing but a title and a back arrow.

The band these pages wore before the flexible bars was pinned in dp instead: one title line,
one subtitle line and 8dp above and below, 56dp at scale 1.0 — the height the fixed strip went back
to, derived from the two styles' own line heights rather than written down. It existed because
the heights *moved*: measured on the emulator, the chat page's band was 236 px, 63 px of status
bar and 173 px of content, and it changed whenever the title or the subtitle did. A Han glyph
needs more of a line box than a Latin one at the same style (`新对话` laid out 82 px where
`run ls /nonexistent-dir-…` took 74), so the band depended on the script its title happened to
start with; the chat page's title is user data and its subtitle is the agent's state, so both
changed several times a session and everything below moved with them. The flexible bar answered
that by being allowed to move, and the fixed strip answers it by holding one line each and
refusing to be measured by its content at all.

Two rules the header keeps from the band, because they are about the *content* of the line
rather than about the box:

- **The title is one line, ellipsised.** It was `titleLarge` over up to **two** lines, and that
  ceiling was itself a fix: at font scale 1.8 a 71-character title drew as `run ls /nonexist…` in the
  649 px the chat page's three action buttons leave, two lines fitted 12 characters a line at that
  scale, and a third line was 100 px of band — a quarter of the screen. A long title now ends in an
  ellipsis, and the whole of it is in the history list, whose row gives it two lines, and in the
  transcript under the header. The collapsing bar used to be where a title was allowed its two
  lines, because that bar gave the height back as the reader scrolled; there is no such page now.
- **The subtitle is one line too.** It is where a multi-line pi failure used to go; the first line
  names the cause and the rest is on the Agent page.

The chat page's empty-conversation title is the application's name rather than "New conversation":
the bar says *what* the reader is looking at, and "新对话" answers a question nobody asked, since an
empty transcript is what a new conversation looks like — and it is what the hero empty state says
instead, one screen down.

## A divider is between rows, not beside a text field

The search page's key section drew a `SettingsDivider()` over each of its seven credential
fields — six between the boxes and one under the note — and thin lines appeared between the
input boxes. A divider spans its card's full width; an `OutlinedTextField` is inset 12dp
inside that card and rounds its own corners, so the line crossed the 4dp of padding above
and below the box and stuck out past both of its corners, with 4dp of air on each side of
it. Seven boxes with a full-width hairline threaded between every pair read as a layout
fault rather than as a list. The same thing happened in miniature beside the two number
fields and the two SearXNG fields, and those went with them; the fields themselves are gone
from the page now — every credential is typed into the document editor — but the rule stands
for the fields that remain.

**Since the Miuix rewrite there is no divider on these pages at all.** The library's preference
rows carry no hairline — a group is a `Card`, and its rows touch — so the row-to-row half of this
rule is moot rather than violated: nothing draws a second boundary between a switch and a
statement of fact. What survives is the half that is about *fields*. A `TextField` is not a row;
it brings its own box, and it follows the row above it directly. `SettingsTextField` insets that
box **12dp** inside the card — with the box's own inside margin reduced by as much of it as it can
spare, floored at 8dp, so the *outline* stops 24dp from the window while the *label* keeps 8dp from
that outline — and 4dp above and below. The inset has had three answers now: 12dp with a
compensating gutter on the text, which moved every field's text to the right of the rows it sat
among and was removed; 6dp with the label and the outline both kept in their places, after which
the report came back that the boxes still ran to the edge; and 12dp, which leaves the card's own
fill visible down both sides of every field. The 4dp it costs the label — 20dp inside the card
against a row's 16 — is the price of that clearance, and it is taken on the *outline* rather than
on the text. A run of fields
is separated by their own outlines and by the 8dp that two `vertical = 4.dp` paddings add up to. The
section's label and its card are what say where a group starts and ends, and the model and agent
pages had been built field-after-row from the start — the search page was the outlier.

The same paragraph is where the **focus ring** is decided: every field on these pages — and every
field in the app — draws with `piFieldColors()`, which states the library's focus border as the
field's own fill so the 2dp `primary` ring animates to nothing (§13).

The rejected design was insetting the divider to the field's own 12dp so the two would line
up, which is what Material's list insets look like. It does not answer the complaint — the
line is still a second boundary drawn 4dp away from the field's own — and it would have been
the only inset divider in the app. The rewrite made the same argument the other way round: it
removed the line instead of moving it.

## A card's last row is the row that acts

`PiSection` draws its rows in a `Card`, and the retired frame clipped its content to the card's
shape — so a row's press ripple was a rounded rectangle at the card's own corners and a plain
rectangle everywhere else, and which of the two a row got depended on whether anything was drawn
**inside the card** below it. The search page's
*添加配置项或 Key* row showed it: its outcome note (`已保存…`) was inside the card
under the row, so as soon as an option had been added the row's ripple changed shape — square
along the bottom edge — while the storage page's *添加文件夹* row, which never had anything
under it, kept its rounded one.

The rule that came out of it: **the outcome of an action lives outside its group**, below it, so
the group is the list and the one row that acts on it. `WebSearchConfigOptions` and
`WebSearchResetSection` are both built that way. The two explanations that used to sit above the
configuration group are gone entirely (§13): the field's own help line carries what a value must
look like, and a paragraph over a list is the kind of text this app stopped writing. The
alternative — clipping the row to a rounded rectangle of its own so its ripple
never changes — was rejected because it makes a row in the middle of a card look detached from
it: the storage page's row would still not match it in the empty state.

**That particular failure cannot recur, and the rule stays anyway.** The retired frame clipped
every row to `PiShapes.row` and padded the card 4dp above and below its first and last row, so a
row's outline was a concentric rounded rectangle whatever was drawn around it — a row at the foot
of a group was no longer the row that inherited the group's bottom corners. Miuix's `Card` clips
its content with its own squircle surface and the rows inside it draw no press outline of their
own beyond the card's fill, so the shape a row gets no longer depends on what is under it at all.
Measured on the search page before the change: the add row's press outline changed after the first
option was added, because the outcome note appeared inside the card under it, while the storage
page's add-a-folder row never moved. The measurement is kept where it is worth keeping:
`WebSearchConfigOptions` still records it, and the layout it argues for is still right for the
other reason — **a group is rows, and a sentence is not one**, so an outcome is a toast
(`LocalPiToast`) or a note in a card of its own, and the acting row belongs last.

## A list is a card, a row wraps before it clips, and an empty list says so

Three rules the rows and lists share. The Files tab followed the first one for a while
and then stopped, deliberately — see below.

**A list is a card.** Rows sit in a `PiSection` — a Miuix `Card`, inset 12dp from the page by
`PiSection`'s own padding — and the rows inside it touch: the library draws no hairline between
them, and the card's rounded surface is what separates one group from the next. §13 is where the
group's frame and a row's own shape are decided. A row's title sits at the card's own inset, and
there is no leading mark to place — Miuix's preference rows have no such slot — so the column
arithmetic this paragraph used to carry is retired with it: a 24dp mark and a 16dp gap put a title
**76dp** from the screen edge, measured at density 2.625 on the 1080 px emulator where that node
started at x=201, while the section label above it and any note under it sat at x=95, 36dp. What has
not changed is why the frame exists at all: a settings row is a *control*, and a card says "these
belong to one subject". The conversations list was the first to be fixed, and its comment records
what the flat version looked like: "no corners on the tap target, and a full-bleed hairline that did
not match the settings pages the rest of the app reads as one with".

**The rule is about *peer rows of a control surface*, and a listing is not one.** The Files
tab was drawing a full-bleed hairline and square tap targets, was given the settings frame,
and has now given it up again for a reason the rule does not cover: settings rows are a set
of *controls*, where a frame says "these belong to one subject"; a directory is a *table of
objects* whose rows are read against each other by column — name, kind and date, size — and a
card around it makes a listing of two thousand entries look like one panel with a table
painted on it. What it kept from this section is the part that was about the rows rather
than the frame: a 12dp page inset, a rounded press outline, and no hairline at all — the row's own
rhythm separates it, where a settings group needs the card's surface because its rows are the controls
of one subject. §13's Files entry has the rest of the page.

**A row's text wraps before it clips.** `SettingsRow` allows its title two lines, its
subtitle three and its value two, and the subtitle's three are a measurement rather than a
preference: the backup page's category sentences are a list inside a sentence — 40
characters, `主题、语言、工作目录、工具调用守卫、共享存储授权，以及本应用保存的其他全部偏好`
— and two lines cut the end of that list off at any text size above the default. The value
column's own cap is two lines with the same reasoning at one line. A row that carries a
*machine* string — a directory path, a revision — is handed in as the subtitle rather than
as the value, because the subtitle has the row's full width and a third of a row cannot
show a path. A row's *height* is a floor as well as a consequence of its text: the sheet
a picker's rows and the folder tree the storage page draws for itself are the same row as every
other row on the page — an `ArrowPreference` inside the sheet's own card — and that is deliberate: a
listing of folders inside a sheet is where a *second* row component is easiest to invent, and the
tree had one. It claimed to be the same row as the two rows above it while measuring 44dp, 8dp under
its twin and under the 48dp a touch target needs, so every plain folder in the tree was the small row
in its own list. The floor is Miuix's now — one row component, one height — which is why there is no
`PICKER_ROW_MIN_HEIGHT` for two lists to keep in step any more.

**An empty list says so.** A `listFiles()` answer that is empty and one that was refused
are the same empty list, so the Files tab draws one sentence for both; before it drew
nothing at all, which is the one answer that cannot be told from a failed load. It says a
*second* thing now, for the second way a listing can be empty: a filter that matched
nothing in a folder that has rows names the query and offers the way back, because "this
folder is empty" over a folder the reader can see is not empty is a sentence about the
wrong thing.

## A form that holds edits asks before it leaves

The model form is the one page in the app that keeps its edits until Save, which is also what
makes it the only page that can lose them. Every way out — the back arrow, the Cancel button,
the system back gesture — goes through one `requestLeave`, which leaves directly when the
form matches what is stored and otherwise asks (save / discard / cancel). The gesture is the
page's own handler, and it is one line: `PiPage` registers a `BackHandler` whenever it is given an
`onBack`, so every page but the tab root passes one and the model form passes `requestLeave` —
the arrow and the gesture are the same action by construction rather than by two call sites
agreeing. A Miuix picker is a window of its own and takes the gesture before the page behind it,
which is the ordering the old shared handler had to argue for. The comparison
is field-by-field against the stored profile rather than a `dirty` flag set in every writer,
and it counts the model-id field's own draft: "added a model, swiped back, and the model was
not in the list" was an id typed into the field and never added, and a flag maintained by the
eight setters would have had to be right about that too.

**What the question offers depends on whether the form can be stored at all.** It used to
offer Save and Discard unconditionally, and with a profile that was not yet complete — no
provider, no model ticked — that was a loop with no exit: Save was refused, the reason was
written at the bottom of the page the dialog was still covering, and the user was back at the
same two buttons. The refusal is now `refusal()`, a pure function that both the dialog and
`save` ask, and when it answers the dialog's first button is *keep editing* and its body is
the reason; Save appears only when Save would succeed. `refusal` is pure for a reason of its
own: `save` adds the model in the id field as it stores, so a check that mutated the form
would make the *question* change the thing it is asking about.

## The update check is a row, and it is the app's only request of its own

About PiKit gained a **Check for updates** row, and the design question was not how to check
but whether to. A release check on launch would be one request per cold start, from every
install, carrying an install timestamp to GitHub — telemetry by another name, in a project
whose whole claim is that it has none. So the row is inert until it is tapped, the answer is
shown in the row it was asked from, and there is no update *notification*, because a
notification is a check nobody asked for. Four details are each a decision:

- **It asks GitHub Releases, not a version file of the app's own.** The release workflow
  already creates the release and the tag, so the answer is the artefact a user would
  install. This is the distribution channel: Play is not one — the app targets `targetSdk
  28` (see §1) and holds `MANAGE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE`, which Play's policies do not accept for
  this kind of app. `pikit.repository` is the one line that says which repository to ask.
- **It compares `versionName`, not `versionCode`.** Android's integer ordering is a fact
  about installability, not about which release is newer to a person, and the tag is derived
  from `versionName` anyway: `0.10.0` is newer than `0.9.0`, which a string comparison gets
  backwards.
- **It asks the release *page*, not the API.** `GET api.github.com/repos/…/releases/latest`
  is unauthenticated here — there is no token in this app, by design — and GitHub allows
  **60 such requests per hour per address**, so an address behind a VPN can already have spent
  a whole node's budget: measured from exactly such an address, `HTTP/1.1 403 rate limit exceeded`
  with `X-RateLimit-Limit: 60`, `X-RateLimit-Remaining: 0` and `X-RateLimit-Used: 60`. The page
  `https://github.com/<owner>/<repo>/releases/latest` carries the same fact as a `302` to
  `…/releases/tag/v0.2.0`, against no API budget, and the URL it lands on is both the tag to
  compare and the page to open; a repository with nothing published answers `404` (measured
  while this project had no release) or redirects to the releases list (measured on
  `octocat/Hello-World`).
- **It installs nothing.** Downloading and relaunching an APK from inside the app needs
  `REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES`, a `FileProvider` root and a `PackageInstaller` session, and it
  only works when the new build is signed with the same key as the installed one. The row
  opens the release page in a browser instead; the user installs it, which is one tap more
  and three failure modes fewer.

Its failure text is the exception's message — or a named refusal — rather than a guess:
"Unable to resolve host" is a different problem from "GitHub refused the request (403)", and
the person reading it is the one who can act on the difference. `data/UpdateCheck.kt` is the
whole of it: no library, one `HttpURLConnection` that follows the redirect, and two pure
functions (`releaseFrom`, `compareVersions`) that the JVM suite pins.

**The row is one status line, and the page carries no standing paragraph under it.** A
`SettingsNote` used to sit under the application section and state the three facts above in
three sentences — asked only when tapped, opens the release page, downloads nothing — on the
grounds that a page which names the traffic it makes is what lets the rest of the app claim
it makes none. That note was the second thing to read after the row it described, and it
repeated decisions this chapter already records; the row's own idle line is the one-line
answer ("到 GitHub 查看新版本"), and a check that *fails* is the only case that grows a
note under the section, carrying the exception's message. The idle line is written to a
one-line budget for the same reason the failure reason is no longer in the subtitle: the
row must not resize when its status is read. A check that *fails* is the one case that says more
than a line, and it says it in a dialog rather than under the section (`SettingsAlert`): the
reader asked for the answer and it came back wrong, so it is announced and dismissed, with the
exception's own message as the body.

## The About page's rows, and the two that left it

**A chevron has one size on both sides of the app.** The About page's update row drew
`KeyboardArrowRight` at `Icon`'s own 24dp while the settings tree's rows — `SettingsRow`, which
is what the settings tab is drawn from — have always drawn it in a 20dp box. Measured on the
420dpi emulator, the same mark was **19x31 px** of ink on one page and **16x27 px** on the other,
and the report that found it is `检查更新的右箭头和设置首页的右箭头粗细不一致`. The design
system's row drew its own 20dp chevron box and the About page's hand-drawn one matched it; 20dp is
the right answer of the pair — this is chrome beside body text, and the row itself is the target.

**Since the Miuix rewrite there is one arrow and nothing to size by hand.** A row that opens
something is an `ArrowPreference`, which draws the library's arrow inside its trailing slot
*after* whatever the row's `endActions` put there. The About page's update row is therefore an
`ArrowPreference` whose value is a `SettingsValue` and whose arrow is the library's own, and the
two arrows this paragraph was about cannot disagree because one component draws both roles.

**The image revision row is gone.** It reported a digest of the image builder's inputs —
`x86_64-974c4c37820b7790` — and it was a *second* row for the fact the Termux environment row
above it already carries: the digest moves exactly when the bootstrap tag and version beside it
do, so a reader could never act on the difference. §3 is the chapter it was read against, and
that reading happens in the builder, not on a phone.

**The licence and the credits are paragraphs, not containers.** Each was a card whose whole
content was one sentence, which is the one kind of surface §13 reserves for a control or a
subject with a body — a box drawn around a paragraph is a box whose only job is to be a box. They
are `SettingsParagraph`s on the page's own colour, under a `SmallTitle`: 28dp, the column the group
labels use, in Miuix's `body2`, no frame (`许可证和致谢两项的内容文字都去掉背景色，更简洁`).

## The icon choice is a component switch, because that is what a launcher icon is

The personalization page offers two marks — the white field the app ships with, and
the black one — as two `RadioButtonPreference` rows in a card, and it is the only choice in the app
whose effect the app does not draw. `LauncherIcon.DEFAULT` is the white one and the manifest declares its alias enabled,
so a fresh install shows white-on-black and writes no component state at all; the enum lists it
first for that reason. Android reads
a launcher icon out of an app's **manifest** and has no API that changes one at run time, so a
choice between two icons is a choice between two *entry points*: the manifest declares one
`activity-alias` per icon, both targeting `MainActivity`, and exactly one of them is enabled.
`LauncherIcon` names the two aliases and `applyLauncherIcon` is the switch, and that is why the
activity itself carries no `MAIN`/`LAUNCHER` filter any more — with one it would be a third
home-screen entry beside the aliases.

Both marks are visible at once rather than behind a picker sheet, and that is the same argument as
the three themes above them: a set of *names* is better read at once than one at a time behind a
scrim, and the row that was just tapped is the row that changes when the choice lands — the live
preview the sheet hid behind its own scrim. One thing a picker can do that a set of rows cannot,
and it is worth naming: a picker carries a paragraph under its list, where a group of rows can only
end. The page uses neither — the two marks are named by their colours (`launcherIconBlack`,
`launcherIconWhite`), and a third line about the home screen would be prose on a page that is one
choice per row.

The packaged APK is the check rather than the source, and it is cheap: `aapt2 dump xmltree
--file AndroidManifest.xml` on a debug APK reports `pi.kit.mob.ui.LauncherLight` with
`android:enabled=true` and `@mipmap/ic_launcher_light`, `pi.kit.mob.ui.LauncherDark` with
`android:enabled=false` and `@mipmap/ic_launcher`, and the `activity` element with no
intent filter at all.

Four details are each a decision:

- **Enabled before disabled.** The two calls happen in that order because the other one leaves a
  moment in which neither alias is on, and a launcher that notices has just been told the app
  has no entry point.
- **`DONT_KILL_APP`.** Switching cosmetics is not a reason to tear down a running agent, so the
  process is not killed for it. The cost is real and is recorded here rather than left as a
  surprise: the task the change was made from belongs to an alias that is now off, so the launcher
  is free to re-create the icon somewhere else on the home screen.
- **Only the entry moves.** The `activity`'s aliases move; `<application android:icon>` is fixed at
  build time to the white field, so the recent-tasks card, the app's own page in system settings
  and the uninstall dialog always show the app's shipping identity rather than the preference.
  Those surfaces have one icon and no per-entry notion, and the alternative is the app rewriting
  its own identity rather than offering a preference.
- **A fresh install writes nothing.** `getComponentEnabledSetting` answers
  `COMPONENT_ENABLED_STATE_DEFAULT` for a component nobody has touched, and that means "whatever
  the manifest declared" rather than "enabled" — so the enum carries each alias's declared state,
  and the first launch of a new install makes no call at all. That same call is what re-states
  the choice on every later launch, where it is a no-op unless the installed aliases have
  drifted from the preference.

The two icons are one mark, and nothing in the platform can make one vector take another's
geometry: `ic_launcher_foreground_light.xml` is `ic_launcher_foreground.xml`'s path data
character for character, and the two files differ in `android:fillColor` alone. A second copy is
a thing that drifts, so `tools/render-icon.py` compares them on every run — in both modes, not
only under `--check` — and refuses to write anything when the path data, the `fillType` or the
group's translation disagree, or when the white icon's ink *is* the field it is drawn on. That
last case is the one the comparison has to catch rather than describe: a white mark on a white
field is a blank square, and nothing about either file alone says so. The README's picture is
derived from the *shipped* icon — the light foreground and `ic_launcher_background_light` — so the
file at the top of the README is what a fresh install shows, hairline border and all.

## The theme is three rows of names, and the palette is not a setting

The personalization page's **Theme** group is three `RadioButtonPreference` rows — follow the
system, light, dark — and nothing else. The **Accent colour** group that used to sit under it is
gone, and so is the wallpaper switch inside it: the interface is drawn in Miuix's own light and
dark schemes, so there is no hue for a user to pick and no palette to derive. `PiKitMiuixTheme`
passes no `keyColor` at all and lets the controller answer with `lightColorScheme()` /
`darkColorScheme()`; §13 records why that is the point of the rewrite rather than a loss.

What the removal costs is the live preview the swatches were: eight discs were a colour picked by
eye and applied to the page that had just been tapped. What it buys is the thing the rewrite was
for — a page assembled from library components is drawn in the colours those components were drawn
for, and every role (the container steps, the disabled pairs, the divider, the slider's track) comes
with them. A derived scheme is a different palette wearing the same component shapes, which is
exactly the drift the Material 3 removal was about.

Three modes remain because light/dark is not a palette question — it is the one part of the colour
that follows the *user's* context rather than the app's design, and a mode is a *name* ("follow the
system" is not a colour and cannot be shown as one). The three are a set better read at once than
one at a time behind a scrim, which is the same argument the launcher marks use.

The eight colours the app still owns are the avatars' discs (`AvatarColor`), and they are *content*
rather than scheme: a red avatar stays red in both schemes, the way a photograph does. That is why
the enum outlived the accent picker it was named for, and why the avatar sheet keeps the swatch row
the theme page lost.

## The transcript's avatars are a switch, two names and two pictures

The **Avatars** group is a switch over the pair and two rows, one per speaker, each opening a sheet
to set a picture, a short label and a colour. The switch is *over the pair* rather than one per
side: they are one feature — "draw who is talking" — and a reader who wants them off wants both off.

The two fallback marks are drawn by the app, not stored, and they are deliberately different: the
agent's is the **Pi logo** — the same glyph the launcher icon and the setup screen draw, from
`ic_pi_mark.xml`, which `PiAvatar` draws directly — and the reader's is the person silhouette,
a Miuix glyph. A blank label with no
picture falls back to its *own* mark, so the two speakers never fall back to the same thing — which
is the whole point: the avatar is a *name*, and the agent's fallback is already taken. The colour is
one of the eight `AvatarColor` seeds, and the mark itself is a plain circle (`PiAvatar`),
`CircleShape` rather than one of the card's radii because an avatar is the same mark in every app
the reader uses. Its
ink is chosen from the colour's luminance rather than pinned to white, because one of the eight
seeds is light enough that white on it is a squint.

**The two rows, the sheet's 96dp preview and the transcript's 34dp mark are one composable.** They
were two — `SettingsAvatar` here and `ChatAvatar` in `ChatScreen` — and the copies had already
disagreed: the reader's figure was `Contacts` on the row and `ContactsCircle` over a message, so the
same avatar was a plain person where it was chosen and a person inside a second circle where it was
read. `ui/kit/PiAvatar` is the one drawing now, and the speaker is a parameter
(`AvatarSpeaker.AGENT` / `READER`) rather than a bag of flags, because the figures are the page's
decision rather than the call site's. What the settings row shows is therefore what every other
surface draws: the page that lets the reader replace the circle is the page that decides what it is
when they have not.

**A picture may be chosen, and it wins over the label.** The agent's fallback used to be the word
`PI` — two letters standing in for a figure the app already owns — and "PiKit has no photo picker"
is the sentence this paragraph used to carry; both are gone. The sheet's picture row opens the
system picker and `AvatarStore` copies the result into `files/avatars/<slot>.png`, decoded and
scaled once to 512px. The copy is the point rather than an implementation detail: a `content://`
grant from `GetContent` lasts as long as the process that asked for it, so a setting holding the
URI would be a blank circle on the next launch. Re-encoding is the same argument from the other
side — the picked file can be HEIC or WebP, and a restore onto another build has no promise of the
system decoder that read it. The directory is inside `files/`, which is what puts it in the
backup's `SETTINGS` category and inside `SafeDelete`'s territory. A path that no longer resolves
draws the label or the mark exactly as if nobody had chosen a picture.

The label is capped at three characters because it is drawn in a circle the size of a thumbnail,
where a fourth is already an ellipsis. §7.3 has where the marks are drawn in the transcript.

## An action pair is `Small`, on every page

The foot of the model editor — **Cancel** and **Save** — is `SettingsButton`, and **Save** alone
carries `primary = true`; `SettingsAction` is the same kind of control at the row's scale, which is
what the maintenance page's three runs, the backup page's two, the AGENTS.md editor's
Restore-and-Save and the document editor's Revert-and-Save all use. The pair used to be Material's
`Medium`, 56dp against their 40dp, so the two controls at the foot of that one form were a size
larger than every other action pair in the settings for no reason the reader could see. A page's
own actions are one component at one weight rather than one weight per page, which is the same rule
that had already been applied to the model page's own "new configuration" button.

**Since the Miuix rewrite** the two families are the library's: `SettingsButton` is Miuix's filled
button (primary or secondary) for a page's or a sheet's own action, and `SettingsAction` is a
`TextButton` with 12/6 padding for the action at the end of a row. The padding is the whole
difference and it is deliberate: the library's default inside margin makes a row's trailing control
nearly as wide as the value beside it, so three of them on one page read as a toolbar rather than as
each row's own verb.

**A row of actions that is not inside a row is as wide as the page's cards, and its buttons split
it.** This is the rule the banner pages obey, and it is stated once because every footer in the app
takes it: an action that is the only object on its line is a *row of the page* — full width at the
cards' own 12dp gutter — and two actions on one line are halves of it. The backup page's export is
one action (`RunActions` with no dismiss) and its import confirm is a pair, so the two footers on
one page are the two halves of the rule; the model editor's Cancel/Save, the AGENTS.md editor's
Restore/Save, the document editor's Revert/Save and the parameter sheet's Add are the other call
sites, and the parameter sheet's single Add is the case that shows why the rule is worth having —
sized to its word, it drew a button a third of the way across a sheet that had one thing to do.
Before the rule, every one of them was sized to its label and pinned to the trailing edge, which
left two thirds of the page empty beside the page's most consequential control. `SettingsButton`'s
`busy` flag belongs to the same rule: the model form's "fetch" pairs with "add", so a spinner shown
*outside* the button would have narrowed both halves when a run started; it is drawn inside the
button, before its label, where it changes nothing.

## The text size is one multiplier, and its slider commits on release

The personalization page's **Text size** row is seven steps, the second (`0.90`) the default, and
the step is reported as a percentage in the row's own value slot: `SliderPreference` keeps the track
on the row and the number beside its title.

**The default moved one step down, and `M` kept its meaning.** `1.0` is still "the app's own
sizes", which is what makes the row's value read as a share of what the app always was — so a fresh
install now reads 90% rather than 100%. What settled it is the library's own type: Miuix's
`paragraph` is 17sp, and a fresh install at 100% read large next to the apps a phone is held beside,
while 0.90 draws that same prose at 15.3sp. One step and no further, so five larger steps stay above
the default and one remains below it, and so the range's floor is untouched: 0.80 is still 12sp
captions at 9.6sp. Nothing else about the control moved — not the range, not the commit-on-release
rule below, not the row.

**A stored 100% is rebased onto the new default once.** `SettingsStore.update` writes every key on
every change, so an install that had altered any setting already carried an explicit `font_size` —
and a phone that was using the app while 100% was the default carried `m`, which cannot be told
apart from a size the reader chose. Changing the default alone would therefore have reached a fresh
install and no one else, and the reader whose row still said 100% would have sent the same note
again. The store's first read takes a stored `m` as the old default, writes the new step back, and
records that it happened under the `font_size_rebased` flag; from then on the stored step is taken as
it is, so a reader who moves the slider back to 100% keeps it. A restore applies the archive's own
steps either way: a backup written by this build carries the flag, and one written before it arrives
after the running store has already set it.

**Since the Miuix rewrite the sheet is gone.** The size was a row that opened a sheet holding the
slider, and the sheet was the third shape it had had — a slider on the row, then one that expanded
on it. Miuix has a slider *row*, so this control is the same kind of thing as the two choices above
it without a panel: the track is where the alternatives are, and the drag re-measures the page it is
on, which is a better preview of a text size than a sample at 11sp and 20sp had been. Seven
tappable numbers in a group instead would throw the range away — a size is the one setting here that
is not a set of names — and that is why the slider stayed when the other four appearance settings
became rows.

Three decisions, and the second one is a measurement:

- **One multiplier on `LocalDensity`, not nine type styles.** `MainActivity` provides
  `Density(density, fontScale * step)` above the theme, so every `sp` in the app moves together —
  the transcript, the terminal banner, the settings rows and the dialogs (a Compose `Dialog` runs
  in the parent composition and inherits the local). It multiplies the *platform's* `fontScale`
  rather than replacing it, so a device already set to 1.3 keeps that and gains the step on top;
  `density` itself is passed through, because the row above measured its paddings in it. The range
  is 0.80–1.30: the layout measurements in this chapter were taken at font scale 1.8 and that is
  where a row's label starts being clipped, not where it looks best, and 0.80 leaves 12sp captions
  at 9.6sp, which is the floor of what stays legible.
- **The drag moves a draft and commits on release.** Applying each step as the finger passed
  it re-measures the entire interface mid-gesture, and that is what breaks: measured on the
  emulator, a drag across the whole track with nine `input motionevent MOVE`s advanced **one**
  step (80% → 90%), while a single MOVE of the same total distance moved four (90% → 130%). The
  mapping was never the problem — being re-measured under the pointer was. So the slider drives its
  own draft (the thumb, the ticks and the percentage, all of which are the row's own) and the step
  is committed in `SliderPreference`'s `onValueChangeFinished`, which is one re-measure per gesture.
  The draft is keyed on the stored step, so the row follows a change made anywhere else — a restore,
  a build whose default moved — without a hand-written effect, and it does not fight the finger
  while the finger is down.

  The failure mode this guards against is the state outrunning the finger rather than the slider
  being wrong: a value fed back in as the control's own state makes the thumb refuse to move at all,
  which is the failure BOTH sliders in the app shipped with once, and a draft that is the only thing
  `onValueChange` writes is a thumb that never moves either. `SliderPreference` takes the value and
  the callback, so the draft is the value.
- **A row, where the other four appearance choices are rows too.** The theme and the launcher mark
  are visible at once — three rows and two, the current one on a radio — and the size is the only
  appearance choice whose alternatives are a *range*: a picker hiding them until it is opened is
  right for a list of *names* (a language, a model) and wrong for a size, and seven rows of one
  number each is what the range would look like as a list. The row is therefore the shape both
  halves agreed on: the track under the title, the step in the trailing value.

## The search row carries the extension's version

The tab root's `搜索设置` row is the row that names the feature, so it also carries the one fact
about it that would otherwise be a terminal away: the version of the bundled `pi-web-access`
extension, read from the image's own metadata (`BundledExtension.version`) and shown monospaced in
the value column, exactly as the maintenance row shows pi's version above it. On a measured install
that is `0.44.0` beside `1.4.0` for pi and `0.3.1` for the app: three versions, one per thing that
has one, each on the row that owns it. An image without the extension leaves the value empty rather
than printing a placeholder — the search page itself says `searchNotBundled` instead of inventing a
version. The search *page* used to repeat that number as a trailing badge on its master-switch
heading, and it no longer does: the fact belongs on the tab root's row, where a version of a thing
is read, and a second copy inside the page was a number the reader could not act on.

## A value is plain text, and a badge is an action

Three rows on these pages used to end in a filled pill and no longer do: the search page's
workflow and provider rows, and the agent page's `AGENTS.md` row. The pill was chosen as "a value
that also says this row is a control", and what it says instead is *state* — it is the app's action
voice, so a row whose end is a badge reads as a control inside a control. Each of the three is an
`ArrowPreference` with its value in the trailing slot (`SettingsValue`) and the library's arrow
beside it, which is exactly the shape the language row wears — value and arrow on the row. What the
language row's arrow *opens* has been a picker sheet and a floating menu and is a **dialog** now
(`LanguageDialog`): a dropdown off a settings row borrowed a toolbar control's habit, and the
question — which language should the interface speak — is a question with three answers under it, a
confirm and a cancel (§7.1, §13). Since the Miuix rewrite that dialog is a `WindowDialog` holding
the `RadioButtonPreference` rows directly on the dialog's surface and the kit's answer pair. The
rows are full-bleed, and that is deliberate: a `Card` around them inset them 24dp from the dialog's
edge, and a row's press indication is drawn inside its own bounds — so the tap highlight stopped
short of the panel on each side and read as a highlight of the row rather than of the choice. The
answers went through a transparent-container phase for the mirror-image reason (`TextButton`'s
`secondaryVariant` fill merged with the card above it); the card is gone and the answers are the
app's one filled pair, which is what every other dialog draws.

The same distinction removed the version from the search page's section heading, where it was a
filled pill on the one line whose whole job is to name the group — a container inside a label. It
is plain text in the heading's own style now. A pill is not wrong in itself; it means "this is a
value at the end of a row", and neither of these is that. The one place a group's label still
carries a fact is the model form's **Models** heading, which states how many are listed —
`SettingsSection`'s `trailing` slot, the same 28/8 inset and the same subtitle face as
`SmallTitle`, so it is that component with a second slot rather than a second heading style.

Both of that page's standing sentences are gone with the rest of the app's page notes (§13). One of
the two facts was operational — a change to this file takes effect when the agent next starts — and
it was moved into the master switch's own subtitle ("…; changes apply when the agent next starts"),
which is where the reader is standing when they flip it. **That subtitle has since lost the clause
too, because the app no longer needs the reader to know it**: the extension registers its tools when
pi starts, so every write on that page now calls `scheduleRestart` itself — the switch, the added
and removed keys, the restore, and the document editor's save. `scheduleRestart` waits for a turn in
flight before touching the process, so it cannot interrupt an answer, and telling the reader to
restart something the app is about to restart was an instruction with nothing behind it
(`那种提示要重启agent生效啥的你自动重启不就行了`). The other sentence was an encouragement ("you need
not change anything here"), and encouragement is what the manual is for.

The agent page's **working directory** field is the same rule in its other form, and it is the case
that showed where the rule was incomplete: the directory is read when pi is spawned — it is the
child's own `cwd` and `PIKIT_WORKSPACE` — so the field saved as it was typed, the page showed the new
path, and the running agent kept working in the old one until the next launch. It had a sentence
saying so once (`workingDirNote`, still declared and no longer rendered), and then none at all, which
is worse than either: a setting that appears to have been applied. The field now calls
`scheduleRestart` like the web-access writes do, so the debounce turns a typed path into one restart
after the typing stops.

The agent page's context section is the same rule applied twice. Its first row is titled for what
it is — a description of the context (`agentContextLeadTitle`) rather than the first clause of the
sentence it opens — and its second row says `可编辑` in the value slot instead of in a pill. The
Save button in the `AGENTS.md` editor briefly carried "saving restarts the agent" as its own
`caption`, inside its fill; it is gone, because a two-line *Save* beside a one-line *Restore* read as
two sizes for two peer actions. The restart is reported *after* the save rather than predicted
before it, and it is a **toast** now rather than a line of `PiNote` prose under the field — a
sentence about an action that has already finished, which the reader had to clear by typing. §13
records the `caption` parameter's removal and the toast's own vocabulary.

## An action that belongs to a row is drawn in the row

The agent page's process section was a row *and* a strip of buttons under it — `重启 Agent` and
`停止` on a line of their own, right-aligned — and the section's other row put `申请保活白名单` under
itself the same way. Both are now the trailing control of their own row: the process and what you
can do to it are one subject, and a strip below the row made them two objects with a gap between
them (`操作按钮放同一行`). The pair is one button now — *restart* — because the stop was the second
half of the same act made into a separate, destructive-looking control, and a stopped agent is a
state nobody asks for. The keep-alive row's *state* moved with them, from the value
column to the subtitle, and that one is a measurement rather than a preference: the sentence is
thirty-eight characters in English ("Battery optimisation is off for this app") and the ask
beside it is seven characters, so as a value it left the title **79px** — one glyph per line,
`后` over `台…`. A value is a short fact about a row and a sentence about its state is a
subtitle, which is the rule §9.2 already states for machine strings and §13 states for prose.

**The restart button reports itself.** The tap is `stopAgent` then `startAgent`, which is about two
seconds of handshake — and for that whole window the button looked exactly as it did before the tap
while the row's subtitle said "starting", which is a word it wears on the way to `Ready` anyway. A
tap that landed and a tap that missed were indistinguishable (`点击重启agent进程按钮后按钮反馈不
明显`). The control now carries a local `restarting` flag: the row swaps its action for `SettingsWorking`,
a bare spinner in the same trailing slot — the mark rather than the label, and the same 20dp box the
button's own text occupied, so the row does not resize under the finger that just pressed it — which
also removes the second tap that would queue a second restart, and raises `restartedAgent` as a toast
when the start actually succeeded. A failed start does not claim to have happened: it writes
`AgentStatus.Failed`, which the note under this row (`SettingsNote(danger = true)`) is already
showing with pi's own stderr in it.
