# The transcript

*[Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) §7.3.*

## A card per turn: the prompt at the end edge, the answer as a document

The transcript is a card per subject — §13's rule that a card is one subject, applied to
the one list in the app where it holds, because a turn is a subject — and a turn is
**two** of them:

- The **prompt** card holds the reader's own words. It is sized to them and sat against
  the *end* edge, on **`PiTone.prompt()`** — the accent mixed into the card colour, so the
  question is plainly the reader's half by hue while keeping the *same ink* as the answer
  beside it (`onSurface`). It held `secondaryContainer` (a grey — the tone of a control
  rather than of a message) and, before that, `primaryContainer`, whose
  `onPrimaryContainer` ink is white: a bubble that had to be read as a letter rather than
  as prose. A mix is the only fill that is blue *and* ink-compatible with the answer in
  both schemes: 16% of #3482FF into white is #DEEBFF and 34% of #277AF7 into #242424 is
  #25416C, and `onSurface` reads at 15:1 on the first and 7.4:1 on the second. The share
  is chosen from the base's luminance because the *same* translucency cannot be right on
  both: a wash of 14% over the dark page — which is black — landed at #061122, a shade
  that was not blue until it sat beside something blacker. The
  card is a `Surface` that measures its content with `propagateMinConstraints`, so any
  `fillMaxWidth` inside it makes the *card* the full width: the cap and the side come
  from a `BoxWithConstraints` around it, and there is no `fillMaxWidth` anywhere inside
  it. A two-word prompt was once drawn as an 86%-wide bar (`气泡是固定宽度`), and the same
  defect recurred in the opposite direction from a *row* rather than from the card: the
  meta row — then a 28dp copy button and a timestamp — carried a `fillMaxWidth` of its own,
  whose minimum became the card's maximum. "ok" drew as a full-width card, and the cap
  was never the thing that decided the width at all.
- The **answer** card holds the whole of what answered it: the fold band, the reasoning,
  the tool calls and the answer, one card and one subject. The prose inside it is laid
  out as a document, so *that* card fills the measure — `fillMaxWidth` there is a
  statement about the answer rather than about its content.

The prompt card was briefly the quieter of the two: aligned to the *start* edge, on the
answer's own `surfaceContainerLow`, with the reader's own furniture — the image count and
the extras chip, which nothing the model says has — as what identified it. What settled the
return to the end edge and the app's own colour is legibility rather than taste: with the
side, the fill and the width cap all removed, a question and its answer were one surface
step and two cards of nearly the same width, so the transcript could not be read at speed —
a prompt and the reply that followed it were told apart only by reading them. The cost the
start edge had been bought with comes back, and it is two lines rather than the four it
was: a `BoxWithConstraints` for the cap in dp and a `widthIn` to hold it. The cap itself is
kept — 86% of the transcript's measure, so the side stays visible however long the prompt
is.

**The time and the copy button sit under the card, outside it**, and are mirrored between
the two sides: a prompt is `[time][copy]` at its end edge, an answer is `[copy][time]` at its
start edge, so the glyph is always the outer mark and the time is always inboard. They were
drawn *inside* each card's fill, where they read as a second line of the message — a
footnote on the same colour as the words above it, with none of the air a footnote needs —
and the reader asked for them back outside (`时间和复制按钮应该显示在气泡外而非里面`, and the
mirroring is what 0.4.1 did). `MessageMeta` takes the side as a parameter and the card view
draws it once under the frame: for a prompt beside its own card, and for an answer after
`metaItemOf` has found the row the fold already treats as the turn's reply
(`TranscriptRow.Message.showMeta`). Only the outer edge moves, and it moves by the card's own
corner radius (`PiCorner.radius`): the stamp begins where the curved corner has ended. It used to
be pulled out by `(28 − 18) / 2` dp instead — the ink inset of the 28dp copy button that stood at
the outer end of the row before copying became an item in the message menu.

## A prompt's pictures are drawn, from the app's own copy

A prompt that carried pictures used to say `2 张图片` where the reader's own screenshots
were. The line was the cheapest honest answer at the time and it was still the wrong one:
a picture *is* the question, and the one place to check what was actually sent is this
page.

The count existed because pi echoes an attached picture back inside the user message it
persisted — the base64, whole, on `message_start` and in the session file alike. That is
a four-megabyte photo as five and a half megabytes of encoded text, held per turn for as
long as the conversation is open and re-measured on every frame of a scroll; it is why
`ChatItem.User.imageCount` is a number and not a payload. The fix keeps that decision and
changes what the number is *for*: the transcript now carries an **address** per picture and
the app keeps the pixels.

- **The address is a digest of the echoed base64, not of the decoded bytes**
  (`ChatImages.keyOf`). Two paths have to arrive at the same name without meeting: the
  send holds the picked file, the record holds what pi echoed, and a conversation opened
  from disk holds only the session file. Digesting the *encoded text* means the send path
  can name the copy from the string it was about to send and never decode it twice, and a
  pruned cache, an older build or a fresh install reconciles itself by re-deriving the
  name. SHA-1 because this is a filename rather than a secret: 40 lowercase hex
  characters, which is 40 bytes a picture in the transcript instead of 5.5 million.
- **One copy per picture, and it is the picture that was sent** (`files/chat-pictures`,
  written verbatim from the base64 the request carries). The app fits a picture to 3000 px
  on its long side and encodes it before the prompt is built (`SendImage.kt`), and the same
  number is declared to pi in each model entry's `inputLimits`, so pi's own rescale is the
  pass-through branch: the viewer and the model are looking at one picture rather than two
  approximations of it. The directory is pruned on a 256 MB budget, oldest first, and the
  copy is decoded again at the edge each caller draws at — 512 px for a tile, 3000 for the
  viewer.
- **Written at send time, and re-derived from the session file when there was no send.**
  The one moment the bytes are certain is the send (the app has just encoded them), so
  the copy is written in the same `Dispatchers.IO` block that builds the payload and the
  row pi echoes a moment later finds its file already there. A conversation that predates
  this cache — or whose entries were pruned — is filled in by one scan of its session
  file (`SessionTranscript.forEachPromptPicture`), which stops as soon as every picture
  the transcript is asking for has been found, and bumps `ChatPictureCache.generation` so
  the tiles that drew a placeholder redraw.
- **One picture at its own aspect, several as tiles.** A lone picture takes the card's own
  measure and the height that follows, up to 300 dp — a landscape photo is a wide, short block
  and a 9:16 screenshot about 169 × 300 — because it is the message, and squaring it would hide
  the part that was asked about. The first cut of this capped the box at 208 × 156 dp and a
  portrait screenshot came out **88 dp wide**: a thumbnail of the thing the reader was asking
  about, in a card three times its width because the sentence beside it was longer. Two or more
  are square tiles in a two-column grid, half the card's measure and no wider than 150 dp,
  cropped: a set of pictures is *identified* by them rather than read. Both frames are sized
  from the constraints (`BoxWithConstraints`) rather than asking for `fillMaxWidth` — inside this
  card that would become the card's *minimum* width.
- **The count line survives for the case it was always right for.** A picture whose copy
  is gone draws the count, not four empty frames and not an error: a cache miss is a state
  the page shows. The card asks for it only when *none* of its pictures can be read, so a
  prompt with one pruned picture and one present draws the pictures.

The viewer is the one surface in the app drawn in a platform window rather than one of
Miuix's, and the reason is shape rather than taste: `WindowDialog` is a card by
construction — `DialogContentLayout` pads its panel by the status bar's inset, insets it by
`DialogDefaults.outsideMargin` and cuts it with the screen's own corner — so a viewer built
on it would leave a strip of the conversation above and around the photograph.

**The picture is what opens, not the window.** Every tile in a set records its node and
the bitmap it is already drawing when it is laid out, and a tap turns that into one
rectangle on screen per picture (`screenRect`) — not only the tapped tile's, because the
viewer swipes and the closing flight has to land on whichever picture is on screen. The
viewer's first frame draws the picture in exactly that rectangle, with the tile's 8dp
corner; the rectangle then interpolates to the fitted picture while the corner eases to
none (`PictureFlight`). One *rectangle* rather than a
scale plus a clip, because a grid tile is a square crop of a portrait photo: the picture
keeps its own shape inside that rectangle and the rectangle clips it, so the first frame is
the tile exactly and the last is the whole picture — a fitted rectangle and the picture have
the same shape by then, which is where the crop necessarily disappears. The ground starts
transparent and fades to black behind it, which is
why the window is a `Dialog` with `decorFitsSystemWindows = false` whose platform dim is set
to zero: the dimming is the viewer's own layer, drawn at an alpha that follows the finger.

**The window is reached from inside the content.** A viewer's ground and its dim are both
window-level, and `LocalView.current` read in `PictureViewer`'s own body is the *activity's*
view: its parent is a `ComposeView`, so `(view.parent as? DialogWindowProvider)?.window`
answers null and every window-level setting is skipped without a symptom — until the window
is removed. That is what made closing flash. The dialog window keeps the theme's dim behind
itself and over the page, so the page sat about 40% dark for as long as the viewer was up and
jumped to full brightness on the frame the window went away. The fix is to read `LocalView`
inside the `Dialog` content and then clear `FLAG_DIM_BEHIND` and set `dimAmount` to zero: the
black ground is this composable's own layer and follows the finger. Measured frame by frame
through a 10× slow-motion close, the page now ramps 138 → 234 with no step larger than 5,
where it used to step 101 → 235 across two frames.

**A downward drag closes it.** One finger on an unzoomed picture that moves vertically is the
dismissal: the picture follows the finger, the ground and the chrome fade
(`PictureFlight.groundAlpha`, `chromeAlpha`), and releasing past 120dp — or faster than
900dp/s, a flick the distance alone does not capture — plays the same flight backwards onto
the tile. A horizontal drag is still the pager's, and a pan while zoomed is still the page's,
so the three gestures share a finger without a mode. The finger's own offset is part of the
flight rather than a transform on the leaving page: a viewer closed by dragging takes over
from the pager exactly where the finger left it and then travels that distance back, where
leaving it out snapped the picture up by the drag — 350px for a 350px drag — before it
started to shrink.

**The flight ends on the frame it lands on.** `animateTo` returns during the frame its last
value belongs to, so removing the content in the same breath skips the frame at rest: the last
picture a reader sees is the previous one, 3–9% larger than the tile, and the tile replacing it
is the step that reads as the picture jumping once it is back in the message. The exit waits
two frames (`withFrameNanos`) before it hides. Measured on a slow-motion close, the last drawn
frame is now `p = 0.0` with a rectangle equal to the tile's — `(421, 451)-(864, 1239)` against
the tile's own — and the window is dismissed 28ms later.

The platform dialog added a second, later step in the same place: with the flight already at
`p = 0.0`, the window's own exit animation still scaled its content for two frames — the
picture's top edge moved 452 → 446 before the window went — and that is the jump a reader saw
once the picture was back in the message. The viewer's window is therefore given no animation
of its own (`setWindowAnimations(0)`): the flight is the whole of its motion, so the landing
frame is the last thing it draws. Measured again on a slow-motion close with the platform's
window animations enabled, every frame from the landing on is the tile's own rectangle.

The system bars are **left alone**, with light icons, rather than hidden. Hiding them was
the previous viewer's choice and it is the one thing that cannot coexist with the flight:
removing the status bar re-lays out the activity, so the tile whose rectangle the flight was
measured from moves by the bar's height while the viewer's window does not — the picture
would close onto a rectangle the tile no longer occupies.

The chrome is two rows, one at each edge, and it is the app's own **glass disc**: 42dp,
`PiGlass.fill()`, `onSurface` ink and a 22dp glyph, the same object as the conversation
header's marks (§13). The first version of the viewer used a dark scrim instead — 38% of
black with white ink — on the argument that a photograph is not a page. It is legible, and it
is a second family of controls for one screen: the reader learns a set of discs on the
conversation page and meets a different set over a picture. Over a white screenshot the
capsule's edge merges with the picture and the glyph is what the reader aims at, which is the
same trade the header makes over a light page. The three marks buzz through `tap()` for the
same reason the header's do (§16).

The top row is the ✕ at the leading edge and the position in the middle, which is a caption
for the set rather than a control. The foot carries **Save** and **Share**, the two things a
reader does with a picture they did not draw: `MediaStore` for the gallery and the app's
`FileProvider` (or the picker's own content URI) for another app. A tap shows or hides both
rows; closing is the ✕, the platform's back gesture, the back key or the drag, all of which
are deliberate — the old viewer closed on any tap, which is why it could never grow a zoom.

Saving is best-effort by design: on Android 11 and later the media database owns a copy the
app writes itself and the insert needs no permission, but on 10 and older it would have to
hold `WRITE_EXTERNAL_STORAGE` — which this app never asks for at run time, since the shared
storage it does use goes through the all-files-access app-op — so `savePicture` answers false
and the chrome says `保存失败` rather than reporting a save that did not happen. Sharing has
no such split: a cache copy goes through the `FileProvider` root the app already declares,
and a composer attachment is the picker's own content URI.

A picture can be **pinched, double-tapped and panned**. A fitted picture is a picture read at
the size the phone chose, and a screenshot's small type is unreadable until it is magnified;
`PictureZoom` holds the four rules — the fitted size, the anchor a zoom turns about, the
offset clamp and the tolerance for a pinch that lands on 1.0004 — as pure functions with
their own tests, and `PictureFlight` holds the flight's, for the same reason. The pager keeps
its horizontal drag while the picture is at fit, and a gesture the page consumes (a second
finger, or one finger while zoomed) is one the pager cancels, which is how swiping between
pictures and panning one picture share a finger without a flag to switch between them. Each
page is decoded on `Dispatchers.IO` at viewer size and released when it leaves composition —
four pictures held at viewer size would be 26 MB of bitmaps for the one on screen.

**The composer's tiles open the same viewer.** A picture picked into a prompt could not be
looked at before it was sent: the only thing on its 64dp square was ✕. It draws the same
tile the send will carry, so tapping it opens the window the sent picture opens — same
chrome, same gestures, same flight — and the only difference is where the bytes come from:
a cache key for a picture pi has echoed back, the picker's content URI for one that has not
left yet (`PictureSource`).

The viewer is **driven by `show`**, like every other window in the app (§13): the target is
held while the flight plays out, and the composable is only torn down when the exit has
finished. It is a platform dialog rather than Miuix's window because of the shape above, but
the contract is the one `PiDialogHost` states.

One known edge, recorded rather than papered over. The `[Image: original 1200x2670 …]` note
is the machinery chip's business and is not drawn as a caption under the picture. A prompt
whose picture this app had to fit carries one written here (`SendImage.kt`'s
`dimensionNote`), in pi's wording, so the model still learns the ratio between the picture
it sees and the file on disk; a picture that needed no fitting carries none, because the
model is then looking at the original pixels. And a session file past
`SessionTranscript.MAX_BYTES` is refused outright (see §8), which image prompts reach much
sooner than text does: the fix there is to move that parse off the main thread and drop each
image block as it is folded, not to raise the number.

## The avatars sit over each card

When the avatars are on (the personalization page's **Avatars** group, §9.2), a 34dp round mark is
drawn **over each card** — above the frame, on the card's own side — rather than beside it. Above
rather than beside because the transcript is a column of full-measure cards, not a chat of narrow
bubbles: a mark set beside the frame would take its width from every line of prose in the answer,
and a side gutter for it is the turn rail this page already removed. The reader's mark is above the
prompt at the end edge; the agent's is above the answer at the start edge, one per turn rather than
one per row, because the answer card *is* the turn and the folded steps inside it are the same
speaker.

The marks are a *name*, not a second cue for the side — the side is already carried by the prompt's
end edge and the answer's start edge — so the two are deliberately different marks (the app's own
PiKit mark and the person silhouette) and both can be switched off, which leaves the transcript exactly
as it was before the names existed. The 8dp between a mark and the card under it is the inside-module
gap, not the transcript's `rowGap`: it belongs to the card's own top edge, and `rowGap` is the
space *between* two subjects. §9.2 has what the marks are, and the name that may be set beside one.

**The name is a caption, and it is on the speaker's own side.** A reader who has given either
speaker a name sees it beside that mark — to the **right** of the agent's, into the answer, and
to the **left** of the reader's, away from the screen edge — in `footnote1` (13sp) and the muted
ink, one line with an ellipsis. It is a caption on the conversation rather than part of it, and a
blank name draws nothing at all: the row is then the mark alone, exactly as it was before names
existed.

**13sp, not the smaller footnote.** The caption was written in `footnote2` (11sp) — the smallest
text the interface owns — beside a 34dp disc whose own drawing is 21dp, and at that size the name
read as a speck attached to the mark rather than as the speaker's name. One step up the same face
keeps it well below the message's 17sp, so it is still a caption rather than a heading.

**One component draws the circle, in all four places it appears**, and that is a correction rather
than a tidy-up. The transcript and the personalization page each had their own copy of the drawing
code, and the copies had drifted where it matters most: the reader's fallback figure over a message
was `ContactsCircle` — a person inside a *second* circle — while the settings row drew `Contacts`, so
the reader's own avatar read as a badge-of-a-badge in the conversation and as a plain person on the
page where they had chosen the rest of it. The luminance rule for the ink, the downscaling of a chosen
picture and the order the answers are tried in now live in one composable, `ui/kit/PiAvatar`, and the speaker is a parameter rather than a flag: which
figure stands for a speaker is the *page's* decision, so a new call site cannot pick a third answer.

**34dp, up from 26.** The mark is the transcript's only non-text element and it repeats on every
card, so it was sized down to stay out of the way — and at 26dp the *figure* was not legible at a
glance. The transcript is read at arm's length, and which of the two speakers a
mark belongs to has to be tellable at a glance rather than by looking for it. 34dp is the size the
agent's mark is drawn at in the process row, and it clears the answer card's own 16dp inset without
competing with the text's first line.

**And the two fallback marks are not the same share of that circle**, which is a correction rather
than a detail. Both were drawn at 62% of it until the mark was looked at beside the silhouette: a
framework icon's *box* is not its ink — the person figure draws between 4 and 20 of its 24 units,
so its ink is 0.62 × 16/24 = **41%** of the diameter — while `ic_pi_mark.xml`'s viewport *is* the
glyph's bounding box: the launcher wordmark's P, three cells wide by four high, 12.6 × 16.8 units.
The same 62% therefore drew the agent's mark at half again the silhouette's weight, so its circle
read as a logo with a ring of colour around it (`pi 字样占比太大了`). The mark is at 46% now, which
is the silhouette's ink, and it still reads as the heavier of the two because it is a solid blocky
figure where the silhouette is the outline of one.

## The header does not move

The page's frame is `PiPage` (§13) — a Miuix `Scaffold` with a small top bar — and its header is
the shell's own strip: one line of title, one line of subtitle, under the status bar. This page's title is the
first thing the reader asked, so it was the one page that took the *collapsing* flexible
bar and gave its height back as the reader scrolled — measured on the 1080 px emulator, the
expanded bar was 399 px (152dp) under the status bar's 63 px. That is gone with the flexible
bars, for the reason §9.2 records: their height was never equal between pages, so the strip
changed height between destinations and, on this page, within a single scroll.

The title is the bar's own one-line title, at Miuix's `title3` size with an ellipsis — the same
size as every other page's, which is the point: the chat header used to ask `PiScaffold` for a
smaller style *while the title was prose* (`对话标题字体应该比现在小很多以显示更多标题内容`), and
that exception disappeared with the custom header. The slot still holds a *name* in two states —
the session's own name when it has one, and `PiKit` itself on a conversation with nothing in it —
and `conversationTitle` is the rule that picks between them.

The **agent's mark** used to stand in the header's action row: an inert silhouette, the app's
own signature, carrying the live tone while a turn ran. It is gone. It was drawn beside the
row's first button, and to the reader it was a placeholder with nothing to say —
`压缩上下文前面…一个无用的占位图标` — while the facts it carried are on screen without it: the
subtitle still states the agent's state in words, and the composer's stop button is still the
"something is running" control. **The row holds two actions, history and new.** It held three until
the compress control that led it was removed: `⌘` already carries `/compact`, so the bar offered
that one operation twice, and its copy was a one-shot command wearing the row that moves between
conversations. §7.1 has the row and the argument.

An empty conversation draws the empty state rather than a blank area: a title and one tip, with no
mark above them. It is the first thing this page ever shows, and a blank list is the one answer that
cannot be told apart from a page that failed to load. It is centred in the transcript's own box,
which is what is left above the composer's band (§7.1), so the hero's last line is never behind it.

The mark that used to stand there had itself replaced a medallion: a 96dp nine-sided cookie on
`secondaryContainer` with a 48dp seven-sided one inside it. That is the app's decorative silhouette
used as a *container*, and at 96dp the pair read as "a shape" rather than as anything about the app
— `莫名其妙的圆形图案`. It was replaced by the app's compact mark at 64dp, drawn without a container in
`primary`, because `ic_pi_mark.xml` is a white glyph and white on a light page is nothing at all
(§13). The mark is gone too now: the title is already the page's statement, the mark the reader just
tapped on the home screen has already said which app it is, and a second copy over the title made
the first screen of a conversation read as a splash rather than as a page about to be filled. The
empty state keeps only what the title cannot say — the tip.

The second line is a **tip from a rotating set** rather than one fixed sentence. It named both
things a prompt can be — a question, or a shell command — and it was the same every time, which is
a line the reader stops seeing after the second conversation. The set costs one line of prose per
entry and tells them something they did not know instead; the `!` tip is first because it is the
one that changes what they can do from this page. One entry names the **two-way swipe** between the
conversation and the list, because the drag is the one affordance here with nothing to find it by:
the clock icon is discoverable, the gesture beside it is not. The entry is picked with
`tips.random()` inside a `remember`, so it is stable while the page is on screen and new the next
time an empty conversation is drawn.

A conversation that is **being opened** is neither of those: it is empty for the length of two
round trips to pi, and the page drew the new-conversation screen over it — a flash of `PiKit` and a
medallion between one conversation and the next. `ConversationState.loadingSession` is what tells
the difference: `switchSessionTo` clears the transcript, raises the flag, and lowers it in a
`finally` once `get_messages` has been folded in, so a switch that throws cannot leave the page
saying "opening the conversation" for ever. While it is set the transcript's place holds a
`ChatStatusPill`, which is the app's own word for something running.

## The transcript stops following when the user scrolls

The list scrolled to its last item on every streamed token, unconditionally. Both
the follow and a finger on the screen write to the same scroll offset, so scrolling up to
read a tool's output was undone a few milliseconds later. The position alone cannot tell
the two apart — it also moves when the follow moves it — so the state machine reads
`isScrollInProgress` and a flag for the frames this app's own `scrollToItem` owns. It is
three rules:

- **The position decides, not the start of the gesture.** Stopping on the frame a
  drag *began* at the bottom looks right, but a phone scroll is a small drag repeated,
  and a first draft then resumed on the next frame of the same drag — invisible to a
  unit test that asserts on one frame, a visible jitter on the device.
- **A gesture's judgement waits for its end.** Mid-fling the offset passes through the
  bottom on its way further up, and the frame the gesture ends on is also the only one
  allowed to write the answer: a streamed token changes the list's content — and
  therefore `canScrollForward` — on its own.
- **Our own scroll is not a gesture.** `scrollToItem` sets `isScrollInProgress`
  exactly as a drag does, so without the programmatic flag every follow would turn
  itself off on its own first frame.

`FollowTailState` is pure and Android-free, for the same reason
`ConversationReducer` is: the symptom of getting it wrong is "it feels janky", and a
screenshot of the bug and a screenshot of the fix are identical. `FollowTailTest`
walks whole gestures rather than asserting on single frames.

Two things about the *scroll itself* were wrong underneath that rule, and they presented as
one bug: a very short flick jumped to a fixed position, and the jump-to-latest button did
not reach the latest.

- **`scrollToItem(last)` is not the bottom.** It aims at the last item's *top*, so an
  answer taller than the viewport was left showing its first line with the rest below
  the fold, and a tiny gesture that re-enabled the follow snapped back to exactly
  that. The caller's own row count is still passed into `scrollToEnd`, because
  `layoutInfo` is empty before the first layout pass — the frame a restored
  conversation is scrolled on.
- **The programmatic flag was a dead local.** `rememberFollowTail` had its own
  `var programmatic = false` that nothing ever set, while `ChatScreen` wrote a
  `MutableState` of the same name that nothing ever read, and the rule read the dead
  one. It works by accident only while every follow lands *at* the bottom — the first
  bug — so the flag is now a parameter.

## The end of the list is whatever the list says it is

The button still did not work, twice more, and both times it came out of *computing* that end
instead of asking for it. The distance left to scroll was measured as
`last.offset + last.size + afterContentPadding - viewportSize.height`, and it
disagreed with the list's own `canScrollForward` by exactly two content paddings:
Compose's measure pass sets `viewportEndOffset = mainAxisAvailableSize +
afterContentPadding`, so the last item has to end *one* padding above the viewport's
end before the list is done. Measured while an answer streamed on the Medium_Phone
AVD, with the button still on screen after a tap:

```
visible=1@-2962+3667  viewport=810  afterPad=105  canFwd=true   (formula: 0)
```

The formula said "at the end"; the list said it could still scroll, and the button
follows the list. `scrollToEnd` now asks instead of calculating: `scrollBy` a
deliberately huge probe and read the delta it *consumed* — `scrollBy` clamps at the
list's own bounds, so what it returns **is** the distance that was left, and the same
measure pass produces both numbers, so they cannot disagree. The animated path uses
that distance by scrolling back by it and then animating over it, with no suspension
point between the two calls. Animating to the last item's *top* instead is what had
produced an up-then-down motion.

The other half of that defect is *timing*: a streamed token changes the text before
it changes the layout, so a jump that arrives with one ends short by that token's
height, and once the answer stops there is no next token to correct it with. A
`settle()` step now waits a frame and re-probes, up to three frames, before the jump
is considered finished.

The transcript also ends one line of blank space above the composer
(`TRANSCRIPT_TAIL_SPACE`, 26dp — one line of the reading style's 17sp/26sp box, where
the constant was 24dp under the old 14sp body): an answer used to end flush against
a full-bleed bar of the page's own colour, so it read as cut off rather than as
finished, and the blank space was the only edge it had to end against. The composer is
a `surface` band with its own edge now, so the *shape* says where the answer stops and
this only has to keep the two from touching — one line of air, where the answer used to
want two.

**The jump control's clearance is measured, not painted on.** It sits 10dp above the
composer band, and the number it is added to is the band's *published* height — its content
plus its foot. The declaration order in the band is what makes that height right:
`onSizeChanged` outside `bandBottom` sees content and inset together, while inside it sees
only the content node and under-counts by the foot — 95 px on the 1080 px emulator (63 of
gesture strip and 32 of air). It was inside, so the disc was drawn 95 px below the band's top
and its lower half sat behind the chip row: the control read as covered, and on a conversation whose end was one
scroll away it read as missing. The same number is the list's tail padding, so one
measurement keeps the end of the conversation and the control that returns to it in the same
place (§7.1).

## One scroll position belongs to one conversation

The list state is hoisted above the tab crossfade (`ChatComposerState`) so a tab switch
does not lose the reader's place, and that is all it was ever meant to survive. It also
survived the *conversation* changing, and the state of a list that is no longer measured
is not a reading of anything: a new conversation inherited the previous one's first
visible index, and `canScrollForward` — a property of the last measure pass — froze at
what the long conversation had left behind. The visible form of that is the
**jump-to-latest control sitting over an empty conversation and doing nothing when
tapped**: it is drawn from `canScrollForward`, and the `LazyColumn` it belongs to is not
composed at all while there are no cards, so there is no list for the control to scroll
and no measurement to make it disappear.

Two answers, one for each half. The position is reset when the session file changes and the
transcript is empty, so an empty conversation is entered at its own start rather than at
another one's offset; and the control is gated on `cards.isNotEmpty()` — nothing is
scrollable about nothing.

## A restored transcript is at its end before its first frame

The report that named this one was a flash rather than a wrong position: opening a saved
conversation drew its *top* for a frame and then jumped to its tail. The follow rule did the
jump, and it runs as a coroutine after composition, so the list had already been laid out
once — and that first layout is the expensive one, because it measures every row and attaches
every formula. Measured on a 50-card transcript, the composition reported the list at `idx=0`
and the follow landed at `idx=45` **255 ms later**; on a shorter conversation the wrong frame
is brief enough to read as a blink, and opening one while the drawer is still sliding over it
is when it is most visible.

`LazyListState.requestScrollToItem` is the fix, and it is a pre-measure request rather than a
scroll: the position is recorded during composition and applied by the next measure pass, so
the frame that first draws the list already draws its end. The index is the last card and the
offset is deliberately past the end (`TRANSCRIPT_END_REQUEST`) — the truthful offset needs the
last card's height and the list's bottom padding, neither of which exists before that measure,
and the list clamps a request past its end to its own furthest position. The same transcript
measured after the change reports, on its first drawn frame, `idx=45 off=428 canFwd=false`.

The flag that makes this once per conversation lives with the list state
(`ChatComposerState.transcriptPlaced`), not with the page: a look at the terminal disposes the
page and not the list, and a reader who had scrolled up comes back to where they were instead
of being returned to the tail.

## A tool call is a step, not a card

A `bash` card's width changed *while it ran*, because the summary line was
`weight(2f, fill = false)` — which lets it shrink to its text — and the chevron
beside it competed for the remainder differently depending on how long the command
was. The fix was a card exactly as wide as the transcript and exactly one text line
tall, with the collapsed output always two lines of monospace. That was still a
*card*: a filled panel with a header row and two lines of output under it, so a turn
that used six tools pushed the answer it was asking for a screen and a half down with
scaffolding. The row is now a **step in the turn's work**, not a card and not a pill. ZCode's agent
transcript draws a step as one quiet line, and that is the shape adopted here: the
family's word (`终端`, `读取`, `编辑`), the argument, and only the state worth saying — a live dot while the call is out, red ink when it failed, and
**nothing** when it succeeded. One text size, one line height and no fill, so six steps read
as six lines of one list rather than six filled capsules. The live dot still answers "is this
still running?" at a glance, which is the one thing the state pill's fill did well; what is
gone is the container that made every call a box, and the cross that repeated a failure the
family word already carries in red.

**The state pill was the design this replaced, and its cost was decoration.** It solved a
real defect — a finished call used to drop the pill for a 22dp bare row, so the same call
changed shape at the instant it ended — by keeping one 28dp pill in every state and swapping
only the mark inside it. But six calls in one turn then drew six filled capsules of different
widths and colours, and the loudest word on each row was the tool's own name. The transcript
around them is bare text; the capsules were the only stack of coloured boxes in the
conversation. The one-shape rule survives, now applied to the *line*: the running and settled
rows have the same height, the same text columns and the same chevron position, and only the
6dp live dot at the end appears or disappears — while a failure changes the family word's ink
to `PiTone.error()` instead of adding a glyph.

**The pill had one measurement worth keeping.** Its mark box was 14dp in every state, because
sizing the box by the mark made a running pill 8dp narrower than the settled one —
`10 + 6 + 5 + label` against `10 + 14 + 5 + label` — and moved the label and the argument
beside it in the frame the call ended. The line does not reproduce the box — a dot that is
only a dot is cheaper — but it keeps the rule the measurement was about: the row's height
does not change with the state, and the label and argument keep their columns.

**The family word is a translation, not a tool name.** pi's wire names are `bash`, `read`,
`web_fetch`, `TodoWrite`; none is a word the interface should make a reader learn. A pure
`toolFamilyOf` maps them to the families the app names, and an unknown spelling — a
third-party MCP server's tool — keeps its own name rather than being filed under a guess.
The precedence matters where names overlap: `TodoWrite` is a todo before `write` makes it an
edit, and `web_search` is a web call before `search` claims it.

**The argument is the line's subject.** A file name is drawn in `onSurface` because it is what
the reader scans for; a shell command keeps the summary grey and the monospace face, because a
command is technical detail rather than a title. ZCode makes the same distinction, and it is
why the row no longer has a name label competing with the thing the call actually did.

**The expanded panel keeps `PiTone.inset()`.** It is still the only filled surface a tool call
has, which is what says "this is the thing you opened" without decorating the closed rows.
The reason the fill is a wash rather than the palette's high step has not moved:
`surfaceContainerHigh` *is* the answer card's own `surfaceContainer` in the dark scheme
(#242424 both), so the panel had no fill there; an inset is a relative step in both schemes
and is the fill the code blocks and the reasoning panel take as well.

**A call the events never ended is finished by hand, as a failure.** The live indicator means
"something is still running", and a card can outlive the thing that would have said otherwise:
pi skips tool execution for an assistant message that ended `error` or `aborted` (its loop
`continue`s past both stop reasons), so the cards such a message streamed are never named by a
`tool_execution_*` record; a process killed mid-call sends nothing at all; and a restored history
has no execution records to read at all — a rebuilt call learns its state from the `toolResult`
message behind it, and one with none never came back. Measured against a provider stream cut
mid-arguments (undici's `TypeError: terminated`): the turn answered after the retries, and the
half-streamed card under the answer wore the spinner for the rest of the conversation. The reducer
therefore closes unfinished calls itself, at four points that each know an end the events will not
report — the message that ended in error or abort closes the calls it streamed, `agent_settled`
(idle means nothing is running any more) sweeps the transcript, the session's process-exit handler
calls `finalizeInterruptedTurn`, and `replaceWithMessages` restores a resultless call as the
failure it is. The red family word is the honest mark for all four: the call did not come back.

The shape never changes with the state: the running and settled rows are the same 30dp line,
the chevron appears where it was reserved, and only the live dot is added at the end — a
failure is the same line with its family word in red. The removal of the
`CircularProgressIndicator` still stands, and the reason has not moved: a second moving thing
beside the text was noise, and a 6dp static dot in the state's own role is enough to say
"not back yet".
The turn's fold chip lost its failure mark at the same time: `failedSteps` is still
computed and still pinned by `ChatFoldTest`, but a red warning and "2 failed" after the
step count made a row whose whole job is "there is more behind this" report a second,
unrelated fact.

## Three rows that open are one shape

The turn band, a message's reasoning and a tool call were three different objects: a filled
chip as wide as its own text, a bare two-part row with the count pushed to the far edge, and
a bare row with a mark and a chevron at the far edge. They are now one family — the same
text size, the same 6dp of vertical padding, the same chevron — which removed
three separate decisions rather than adding one:

- **One row shape**: the words and the chevron where the words end; only a standalone
  reasoning row needs a 16dp brain to say what it is. The turn band has no leading glyph
  at all — its time and step count are a statement about the turn, not another line of
  its work. Inside `StepGroup` the rule is the mark, so a nested step drops its own glyph
  and keeps the same 24dp text column. The chevron never sits at the row's edge: at the
  edge it reads as a second, unrelated control a screen's width from the text it belongs
  to.
- **The chevron is a stroke, not the library's filled caret.** `ExpandMore` is a solid
  triangle-ish mark: at 16dp beside 13sp labels it read as a heavier object than the text it
  belonged to, and a filled triangle is the figure a spinner or a play control uses.
  `ic_chevron_down.xml` is Lucide's chevron — 2 of 24 units, round caps, the weight of
  `ic_check.xml` on the same page — and it is still one glyph that turns 180°, so the state
  is still a rotation rather than a swap. The **jump-to-latest** button took the other half
  of the same correction: it carried this chevron, which is the *disclosure* figure three rows
  above it, and a control that scrolls a list is not a disclosure — it is an arrow down
  (`ic_arrow_down.xml`) in `onSurface` on its `secondaryContainer` disc, because the disc's
  own `onSecondaryContainer` ink (#A9A9A9 on #F0F0F0 light, #7C7C7C on #434343 dark) sat
  below 3:1 in both schemes and made the control look disabled.
- **The turn band lost its fill — and its leading list glyph.** A `surfaceVariant` chip
  across the page is what made a turn boundary look like a different *kind* of row rather
  than the first row of a turn, and a 16dp list icon in front of `工作 47 秒` decorated a
  fact with a symbol for "list" that the indented steps beneath it already say. The band's
  position and its accent-tinted chevron are what separate it from the reasoning row
  directly beneath it. The shape's own rule is §13's: action surfaces are round and
  information-dense ones are not, and a disclosure row is neither a button nor a card.
- **The labels say facts, not verbs.** `工作 47 秒 · 5 个步骤` and `思考 · 1.2k 字` — the
  reference's `Worked 47s · 5 steps` shape, with the time in the interface's own units
  (`秒`, `s`: the units belong to the language and the arithmetic does not, so
  `Strings.Chat.duration` formats whole seconds). "Show reasoning" and the word "已" were
  removed with them: a control's verb belongs in its `onClickLabel`, where a screen reader
  looks for it.

The reasoning row's count is now live while the reasoning streams. It was hidden before, on
the theory that a number changing on every token is noise; it is the only thing on the row
that moves, and it is what says the block being waited for is filling up.

**The band's type is one type.** It used to change with whether the row was a control:
`labelMedium` + `FontWeight.Medium` when the turn hid steps and `labelSmall` + Normal when
it did not. Both cases are the same sentence about the same turn — a turn whose answer needed
no tool call simply has nothing behind the band — and one conversation drew `工作 47 秒` at
two sizes, which is the defect: the same sentence drawn at two sizes. The
chevron is what says which of the two it is, and it is only drawn when there is something to
open.

**A disclosure row's feedback is the control, not the row.** The row spans the card so the whole
line answers a tap, and `clickable`'s default indication is a *bounded* ripple, so on a row that
size it fills the whole width — a bar of colour flashing across the conversation on every tap.
Binding the theme's tint to the row instead was the next attempt and was wrong in a smaller way:
`2 个步骤` is four characters wide, so the highlight became a band across the card with the label
jammed into its rounded leading end and a screen of empty highlight after the chevron, which is
the report that the press feedback's margin from its content was too small.

The tap and the feedback are therefore two nodes. The row owns the tap
(`clickable(indication = null)` over one remembered interaction source) and the tint is bound to
an inner node that hugs the label, through `LocalIndication` — the theme's own colour and shape
rather than a second vocabulary for the same gesture. That node **bleeds 10dp into the card's
16dp inset** on both sides, which is what its horizontal padding costs: the label keeps the
transcript's margin and the highlight gains an edge. Its 6dp of vertical padding is what makes
it read as a control rather than as a line of text with a wash behind it; it was 3dp, and 3dp was
the row's padding rather than the highlight's, so the tint's rounded ends cut into the first
glyph.

## The live cursor was removed

An assistant row used to draw a 2dp caret at the left margin while its answer was on its way:
an `infiniteRepeatable` alpha tween first, then a 2 Hz blink. It was only ever drawn while
the row had no text *and* no reasoning, so what it marked was the wait for a first token,
which is a second or two. The row it sat on already had a progress signal in the reasoning
control's live character count
(`思考 · 1.2k 字`). The composer's stop button is the "something is running" control, and
`drawsNothing()` now drops an empty streaming row instead of holding a blank line open for a
cursor that is not coming.

A *static* bar was the other candidate, and it is worse here: it is the same caret with
the only thing that made it read as one taken away. The blink it replaced is recorded
where the fade is: two alpha writes a second rather than a frame every 16 ms for the
length of a thinking phase, which was the one cost on this page that recomposition
discipline could not remove.

## The turn rail was removed

The turn rail was the reference's turn navigation — a run of ticks down the edge of
the transcript, one per turn, that jumps to it — and it was removed: the
ticks were a second navigation over a transcript that was already scrollable, and
the 26dp they reserved on the right edge were taken from every line of every answer.
`TurnRail` was one `drawBehind` pass over a box the height of the transcript with the
ticks spread evenly, not one composable per turn: two hundred turns would otherwise
have added two hundred layout nodes to the heaviest list on the page, and evenly spread
ticks cannot overlap however many turns there are, while a `Column` of fixed ticks runs
off the bottom of the screen after about twenty.

Three things about it are still the answer if a rail is ever wanted again. Where a tick
pointed was `turnTargets`, aimed at the turn's **prompt** rather than at the fold band
under it — landing with the question just off the top of the screen shows the answer to a
question that is not visible. Its width was reserved in the list's own end padding
rather than overlapped, so no answer was ever drawn under it; the transcript's two
gutters are now the same 12dp and every answer is 26dp wider, while the jump-to-latest
button keeps its 10dp inset from the same corner. And its ticks were not nodes, so the
rail was one target spanning the full height — a 2dp tick is far below the 48dp minimum
touch target — and a tap went to the nearest tick measured from the touch's own y. The
rail was also the only caller of `FollowTailController.stop` — a jump to an earlier turn
is a deliberate move away from the tail — so `stop` was deleted rather than left as an entry
point with no caller.

## The message menu, not a copy button

Every message used to wear a copy button under it, and the transcript was one
`SelectionContainer` from top to bottom. Both are gone. A message's actions are a **long
press on the message**: a panel at the **finger** — not at the card, not at the bottom edge
of the screen — with copy, select-text, regenerate, edit-and-resend and
branch-from-here.

The panel uses Miuix glass through `PiPopupMenu`, with `PiMenuRow` actions, and opens on
`GlassPopup`'s own edge reveal: the panel starts at 15% of its settled width on the
logical end and grows while the rows sharpen. The attached card converts the press to
window coordinates, then the page registers a point anchor with the host shared by chat
and history. This keeps the menu from vanishing if the lazy list recycles the card. The
last message remains available while `show` is false and the panel animates out; closing
rows do not accept another tap.

**The animated clip belongs to the draw pass, not the measure pass.** The reveal keeps
the rows at their settled size and clips them to the growing panel. The first version
built that rounded clip path in the `Layout` measure block, so the path was recorded into
the row layer on the first frame — when the panel was one row tall — and the layer grew
without re-recording it. A four-row menu drew only its first row while the accessibility
tree listed all four, which is why the bug looked like a rendering fault rather than a
layout one. `drawWithContent` now recomputes the path from the animation state on every
frame, and the clip's height is derived from the reveal ratio so it agrees with the
panel's frame.

The long press answers as it is recognised: **a haptic at the long-press timeout**
(`HapticFeedbackType.LongPress`). Every other control on the page acknowledges the finger,
and half a second of holding followed by a silent panel is indistinguishable from a delayed
tap.

**A pressed fill on the bubble was tried twice and removed.** Lit from touch-down it flashed
on every tap — the transcript's most frequent touch announcing itself — and lit only once
the menu was open it arrived together with the panel it was supposed to explain. What a hold
needs to prove is that the gesture was recognised, and the haptic is what proves it; the
bubble is not a button and does not need to look pressed.

**Copy answers with a toast.** The panel closes on the tap, so the glyph cannot turn into the code
block's check; a copy that says nothing is indistinguishable from a tap that missed.

Select-text does not turn on a selection *mode*: it opens the message's Markdown source in
a sheet of its own, selectable and copyable there. Compose cannot select text on the app's
behalf, so a mode would have handed the reader a container and a gesture to discover;
a sheet hands them the text. **The sheet carries no copy row of its own**: the menu that
opened it already copies that same source, so the row was the same action twice, one tap
apart, with the same toast. A selection made in the sheet is still copied by the platform's
own toolbar, which acts on the text the reader selected rather than on the whole message.

- **The copy button was furniture with a control's cost.** It had to be aligned by its ink
  rather than its box (a 28dp target under a bubble, with 5dp of untouchable margin), it
  appeared under every message whose turn had settled, and it did what the selection
  container already did.
- **The whole-transcript container is what claimed the long press.** Compose's text
  selection consumes long-press before any parent gesture sees it, so "long press a
  bubble" raised the platform's own copy toolbar instead of this page's menu. The
  container is now per *message*, and only for the one that asked for it; a tap anywhere
  that is not a control, or a long press on any other message, leaves selection mode.
- **The branch is a file.** pi's sessions are append-only JSONL, so "branch from here" is
  "write the records up to here into a new file with a new session id and open it"
  (`PiAgentSession.forkAt`). The count is of *message* records, which is what the
  transcript's own rows are; a turn that is still streaming cannot be branched, because
  the file it is writing is not finished.

- **Regenerate and edit are the same fork, not an in-place rewrite.** pi's session can
  only advance through a `prompt`, so replacing an answer in place is not a command it
  has. Regenerate copies the records **before the prompt** — `forkAt(0)` is a legal
  branch point, which is what makes the first turn regenerable — opens the copy and sends
  the raw prompt again, pictures included from `chat-pictures`. Edit does the same with
  the text from its dialog; the prompt's file paths are restated in the app's own header
  because their copies are still in `files/attachments`. The original conversation is
  untouched, which is what makes the action safe to try, at the cost of a sibling row in
  the drawer rather than a version chip on one message.

## The selection menu draws no "Select all", and that is two arguments

Tapping an empty part of the transcript to give up a selection made a **"Select all" button appear
by itself and vanish again**. Nothing in this app draws it: on the tap, Compose's `SelectionManager`
clears the selection (`onClearSelectionRequested` → `onRelease`) and then that same tap's own
selection update *ends*, which sets `showToolbar = true` — so the toolbar is requested with an empty
selection, where "Copy" is included disabled and dropped by the platform, leaving "Select all" as the
only item to draw. Captured on the emulator with a 40-frame screencap of one tap: the selection is on
screen for ten frames, the button alone for the next, and the dismissed transcript after that.

The selection itself is not readable from the app — `SelectionContainer`'s controlled form and
`Selection` are both `internal` in this version of Compose — so the menu is the lever, through
`Modifier.filterTextContextMenuComponents` in `ChatScreen.kt`. Removing "Select all" is the fix and
its own argument: the transcript is a `LazyColumn`, and Compose's own documentation for
`SelectionContainer` says a select-all cannot reach items that were never composed, so the control
would take the part of the conversation that happened to be on screen and look as if it had taken
the whole thing. A prompt and the reply that ends its turn each carry their own copy button, which
copies that message's source Markdown — see the section below for why not every message does. The
same 40-frame capture after the filter goes from selection to dismissed in one step, with no frame
in between.

## The copy button and the timestamp belong to the answer, not to every message

Both used to sit under **every** message. A tool-using turn is five or six assistant messages — "let
me look at the file", a tool card, "found it", another card, the answer — and the sentences in the
middle are *fragments* of the answer rather than answers, so one answer wore three or four copy
glyphs and times and the furniture was denser than the text it belonged to.

The row that keeps both is the one folding already treats as the turn's answer,
`ChatTurn.finalReplyKey`. That key is read rather than the rule re-derived, which is what makes the
two views agree: a collapsed turn and an expanded one put the button on the same message, so
expanding a turn adds its steps and does not move the control. A prompt keeps both as well — it is a
message the reader wrote, and its time is the only record of when the question was asked.

Two consequences, accepted rather than overlooked:

- **The "Select all" argument above is now an argument about turns.** The compensation for dropping
  that item was that every message could be copied from its own button, and a *turn* still can, from
  its answer. A mid-turn fragment no longer can — only by dragging a selection over it — and neither
  can a leading assistant greeting, which `replaceWithMessages` deliberately leaves outside every
  turn. `transcriptRows` keeps the row on those greetings for exactly that reason: with one assistant
  message and no question above it, dropping the row leaves no way to copy the only thing on the
  page.
- **A step that fails and is retried loses its own button.** It had one because a failed answer's
  text is what a reader wants out of the app, and that is still true of the failure that *ends* a
  turn — which is the one that keeps its button. A step that failed on the way to a successful answer
  was never the answer.

The rule is one flag on the row (`TranscriptRow.Message.showMeta`), computed in `transcriptRows`
where the turn attribution already is, and read by the assistant row only: a tool card and a notice
draw no meta row either way, and a prompt always wears both.

The stamp itself is a display setting now (`PiSettings.showMessageTime`, on by default). The
personalization page's switch is read by `MessageMeta` before it formats anything; off removes
the stamp under every message and leaves the card exactly as the message wrote it. The switch
hides the time only — never the message — and the history list's own timestamp is a separate
setting (`showHistoryTime`, off by default), because a list of questions and a transcript read
in order answer different questions about time.

The row is drawn at the **foot of the card the message is in** rather than as a row of the
transcript list, which is what the frames settled: the time a question was asked travels with
the question, and everything under a message is part of that message. The two cards sit on
opposite edges — the prompt at the end, the answer at the start — and the copy glyph's own
invisible margin is pulled off in the one direction that keeps its ink on the column the
message's text starts at (see [§12.3](12.3-display-and-marks.md)). The row's pull is what
made the `fillMaxWidth` in it visible: it is the same inset whether the card is full width
or not, so an unwanted one there is invisible on an answer card and is the whole reason a
short prompt drew wide.

**The stamp begins where the card's corner ends.** It is drawn under the frame, and the frame
is a rounded rectangle whose corner arcs are one `PiCorner.radius` (20dp) wide; a line flush
with the card's side therefore starts *inside* the bottom arc's column, which is what made
the time read as tucked under the curve. The flat part of the bottom edge begins one radius
in, so that is where the first glyph stands — on the answer's side from the start, on the
prompt's side the same distance taken off its end. The inset is on the message's own edge
only, because the stamp belongs to that edge and not to the middle of the card.

## The transcript's rhythm: two boundaries and one module gap

Every row was spaced from the next by the same `Arrangement.spacedBy(8.dp)`, and that
one number is what made a conversation read as an undifferentiated stream: the gap
between a prompt and the answer it produced was the same as the gap between two calls
of the same step, so the only thing marking a turn boundary was the text. An answer
streaming before its turn closed had no fold band yet and so sat 8dp under the prompt
it was answering.

The gap is now a function of the two rows — `rowGap` in `ChatScreen.kt`, which the list
applies as `padding(top = …)` with `spacedBy(0.dp)` — and it is computed in the page
rather than in `transcriptRows` so the fold rules stay about what is *shown*. It is the
same function inside a card: the modules of a turn are spaced by it where they sit in the
answer card, and the list applies it between two cards, so the three numbers a
conversation is readable through survived the frames arriving:

| adjacency | gap | why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| last row of a turn → next prompt | 20dp | the only gap that means "new subject" |
| prompt → what answers it | 12dp | one unit, but the two cards must not touch |
| every module inside a turn | 8dp | a thought, a call, its answer, a notice, the fold band |

The first version of the rule had five numbers and a different one on each side of a
tool call — 4dp between two calls, 8dp after one, 12dp before one — so a turn reading
"thinking → bash → thinking" showed 12dp above the call and 8dp below it. What *is*
structural is a prompt opening a turn and the turn that answers it, so those are the
two numbers left, distinct from the module gap on purpose: one number for every pair
is the design this section opened with.

**The same spacing defect came back**, because the module gap is not the whole
of the space between two modules. `AssistantBlock` is *one* row that can hold three
things — the reasoning control, the answer, the error — and the 5dp spacer between
its parts was emitted whether or not there was an answer under it. A step that thinks
and then calls a tool *has* no answer, which is most steps of most turns: the distance
from a tool call up to the thinking above it was `MODULE_GAP` + 5dp while the distance
down to the next call was `MODULE_GAP`. Measured against the two constants: 13dp
against 8dp, in the thinking → bash → thinking shape. The spacer
now belongs to the pair it separates (`REASONING_ANSWER_GAP`) and is only emitted when
something follows it.

The step rail came back, in the one form that pays for itself. The old one was a 2dp
bar down the left of every tool row, with a 12dp inset on *all* of them — it cost every row
12dp of width and moved every tool's mark 12dp right of every other row's text, so it was
removed. The spine now is ZCode's: one 1dp rule at 8dp, a 24dp text column, and only the
work rows of an answer card inside it. The 24dp is an indent from the card's prose margin,
so the work sits one step in from the turn band's label. The band itself carries no glyph:
its time and count describe the turn, while the steps below are the things it opens. A
notice keeps its own program-output rail outside the spine, and the final answer stays on
the card's own margin, which is what makes "what the agent did" and "what it says"
distinguishable at a glance. A group of one row draws no rule: a line beside a single line
of text is a bracket around nothing.

Measured on the Medium_Phone AVD at 320 dpi after the change: the answer card's prose and
the band's label begin at x=56, every step's text at x=104 — 48 px, the 24dp indent — and
consecutive steps are 75 px apart (a 30dp row plus the 8dp module gap).

Two further numbers came from the same review. The prose style is `bodyMedium` with a
22sp line box rather than the theme's 20sp — a 1.43 ratio is under the 1.5 that WCAG's
visual-presentation criterion asks for within a paragraph, and 22sp is 1.57 — and the
transcript column is capped at 560dp and centred once the window reaches 600dp, which
is where 80 Latin characters and 40 Han characters per line coincide. On a 411dp phone
the gutters already put a line at about 55 Latin characters, so nothing is capped
there.

## There is no row entrance animation, and that is the fix

`Modifier.animateItem` was added to every row and taken straight back out. It replays
its appearance animation **every time an item enters composition**, and in a lazy list
that is every time a row scrolls back into view — so the transcript flickered while
scrolling — while its default placement and disappearance springs fought the follow
rule, which is scrolling the same list on every streamed token. The symptom is flicker
while outputting or scrolling, and both halves are one cause.

A message list is the one list where an entrance animation cannot be afforded: it is
watched while it changes continuously, and the only rows that are genuinely new are
the two ends of a turn.

## Reasoning is folded until it is opened

The block used to open on its own while the reasoning streamed and fold itself a
second after the answer began, with a `rememberSaveable` `touched` flag so a tap won.
It was removed: it made every turn taller by a screenful of grey reasoning nobody had
asked for, it moved everything below it twice per turn, and it fought anyone who was
scrolling while it happened. The row's own label carries the progress (`思考 · 1.2k 字`),
so the block is one tap away and nothing moves while the answer above it is being read.

What the label says is a *count*, not a duration. A duration is what the other
implementations show (`Thought for 1.2s`) and the reducer still does not record when
thinking stopped, so inventing one would bill the tool calls that ran before it; the
turn band above the answer already reports how long the whole turn took. The count is
live while the reasoning streams.

The block itself is a filled `PiTone.inset()` panel on the card's inset radius — the wash
every quotation on this page is drawn in, the same fill and the same shape as the tool output
panel beside it, because the two are the same thing: an information-dense inset inside a turn
(§13) — capped at 240dp with its own scroll: reasoning arrives unbounded, and an uncapped
block pushed the answer — the thing that was asked for — off the bottom of the screen. It
used to be a bare block behind a 2dp left rule, which said "secondary" twice and cost an
`IntrinsicSize.Min` row — an extra intrinsic measure of a block of text that grows on every
streamed token, on every frame. The wash replaced `surfaceContainerHigh`, which was "one step
past the answer's card" in the light scheme and *no* step at all in the dark one, where the
palette gives both roles #242424: the panel had no fill of its own there, so the reasoning ran
straight into the answer's card.

**The italic went with the left rule.** The convention it came from is Anthropic's own
"the reasoning as gray italic text", and it is the half of that convention this app
cannot use: the reasoning is mostly Han in Chinese and Japanese, a CJK face has no
italic, and the platform synthesises one by slanting the glyphs — which makes a 14sp Han
line *harder* to read and slants the Latin paths and command names inside the reasoning
in the same pass. Muted colour on its own is the whole signal, and the panel's fill
already separates the block from the answer under it.

The page's three disclosure controls — the turn band, a tool row and the reasoning
row — share one chevron (`DisclosureChevron`): one glyph that turns 180° rather than
two that swap, one size, and the accessibility label on the control rather than on the
arrow inside it.
