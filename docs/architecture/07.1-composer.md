# The composer, and the bottom of the screen

*[Architecture](../ARCHITECTURE.md) §7.1.*

`enableEdgeToEdge()` sets `decorFitsSystemWindows = false`: the window is full-screen,
the IME is drawn *over* the app rather than resizing it, and the app is responsible for
keeping its own content out from under both. Measured on the emulator: the activity
window's frame stays `[0,0][1080,2400]` while the IME window is `[0,63][1080,2400]`
with `mImeHeight = 820`.

## What was tried, and why each one failed

1. **`AnimatedVisibility` driven by `WindowInsets.isImeVisible`.** That flag
   flips several times while one keyboard animation plays, as the IME's insets
   settle, so the bar expanded and collapsed repeatedly and every flip re-measured
   the whole page above it.
2. **`if (!imeVisible)` on top of the padding that already moved the bar.** Two
   mechanisms deciding the same thing: the bar appeared while the page resized in
   the opposite direction.
3. **The bar inside the keyboard's inset**, so it rode *up* and sat on top of the
   keyboard rather than getting out of the way. It shipped twice because it
   *looked* plausible: the bar really did move, just in the wrong direction.

## What is there now

The strip is pinned to the window's own bottom edge, outside the keyboard's inset,
and the **page**, not the strip, is padded by the keyboard: the keyboard rises over
the strip and hides it, and the page above ends exactly at the keyboard's top edge.

```
page bottom inset = max(navigationBars, ime, tabStripHeight)
tab strip         = aligned to the window bottom, inset = navigationBars only
```

There is no visibility state, no animation and no second clock: the strip's
disappearance *is* the keyboard's own motion, so the two cannot disagree. Swiping the
keyboard down reveals the strip progressively instead of snapping it back.

A label on the strip is one line and ellipsised, like every other label in the app, and
it has to be said explicitly: `ShortNavigationBarItem`, the expressive bar's own item,
measures its label with loose constraints, so a label wider than the item **wraps**
instead of being cut. Measured on the emulator in Japanese at font scale 1.8:
`ターミナル` took two lines and grew its item node to 158px against 82px for the three
labels beside it, which pushed the strip's contents out of the strip's own band — the
icons ended up drawn over the page above, and the page's inset is the strip's
*measured* height, so the thing it made room for was the wrong thing.

## The trap: `safeDrawing` already contains `ime`

`safeDrawing` is `systemBars + displayCutout + ime`. Using it as the strip's own
inset therefore added the keyboard's height to the strip's position and pushed it up
on top of the keyboard — design 3 again. Measured with `uiautomator`: the strip's
labels landed at `y = 1454` with the keyboard up, against `y = 2274` with it down — a
delta of 826 px, and the keyboard reported `mImeHeight = 820`. A missing inset and a
wrong inset look identical in a screenshot; they do not look identical in a number.
The fix is `WindowInsets.navigationBars`. The strip's height is measured with
`onSizeChanged` and fed back into the page's padding rather than hard-coded, because
the bar's own band is a Material figure this app does not own: it measures 231 px on
the 1080 px emulator, 168 px of it the bar's items and 63 px the gesture strip below
them, and the page above is padded by that measured total.

## A text field that looked full width and was not

The composer is a full-bleed band with four chips on a line above the field, the field in a
framed box of its own, and a round filled send button on the box's last line. A `Box` with
`weight(1f)` around a `BasicTextField(modifier = Modifier.fillMaxWidth())` fails on the
device: the field measured **312 px wide inside a 780 px pill** — exactly the width of the
placeholder text, so text wrapped there — and everything to the right of that was inert, so
tapping the composer usually did nothing. `BasicTextField` measures its `decorationBox` with
`minWidth = 0` and reports *that* size, so a `fillMaxWidth` chained to it is undone one node
later. The fixed width a parent gives a **weighted child** arrives as a *minimum* and
survives, so `weight(1f)` belongs on the field itself, with no `Box` in between — and the
box the field is drawn in gives it that minimum without a weight at all, because a `Column`
offers each child its own width. It is the number to check the next time this is doubted: on
the same 1080 px emulator the field's own box spans the band's inner width rather than the
width of its own placeholder.

## The composer's control row

The state, the model and the thinking level used to be three read-only rows of the
chat header, which put the three things a user changes most often while talking as
far from the message they apply to as the page allows. They are controls now, on a
row **above** the input box, each opening a modal sheet: thinking level, model, the
context window's fill, and the prompt cache's hit rate. Above, not below: a chip
between the field and the keyboard sits in the busiest strip on a phone screen and
reads as part of the message being written, where a chip above the field reads as
the setting the next message will be sent with. The row is a `LazyRow` so four chips
whose values can each be long stay on one line by scrolling rather than by being
squeezed. A plain `Row` with `horizontalScroll` under `fillMaxWidth` does not do
this: it measures its children against the screen's width and then draws the
overflow past the edge, so the last chip is sliced by the window — measured,
`缓存命中 0%` rendered at x=966..1048 and was cut rather than scrolled to.

The four chips are `PiSelectChip`s — the design system's own chip, drawn at exactly one place
in the app and this is it (§13). Each passes a 14dp glyph through the chip's leading slot and
no `contentDescription`, because the chip's own label is what the control says
(`high`, `packyapi-custom`, `缓存命中 0%`) and the label is the value the reader wants
rather than the noun they can already infer from where the chip sits.

**The chip's fill is the accent's container.** An unselected chip on `surfaceContainerHigh`
was the *same colour* as the composer it sits on, so the row read as four labels with glyphs
beside them rather than as four controls, and the selected state was the only thing on it that
looked pressable. It is `primaryContainer` now — a step away from the band and visibly *coloured*,
with the label on `onPrimaryContainer` — so what distinguishes the row is the theme colour
rather than a step of grey. Two other answers were tried and dropped for the same reason: a
neutral step up (`surfaceContainerHighest`) bought a shade where the problem was a lack of
colour, and a 1dp hairline around that step drew a box around a fill that was already a
boundary (§13). The luminance test the hand-rolled chip before this one carried is not the
same device and was not reinstated either: that one asked the *palette* how far apart two
steps were, and a palette is not a question a call site should be able to ask.

## The band, and the field's own box

The composer is the page's bottom band: a full-bleed `Surface` on `surfaceContainer` — the
same step the tab strip and the root behind it are painted in — in the page's column, under
the transcript rather than over it. The notices sit in the same column, above the band. The
list's own box therefore ends where the band begins, its tail padding is one line of
`bodyMedium`, and the jump-to-latest button needs no clearance from anything: a message
scrolls *to* the band rather than behind it, and the measured height the overlay used to
keep is gone. There is no shadow and no hairline: the fill is a step away from the page's own
`surface`, and a drawn rule here would compete with the transcript's dividers.

The field has a **box of its own**: `surface`, a 1dp `outlineVariant` hairline, and the
scale's `largeIncreased` (24dp) — the step 0.4.1's 22dp `shapes.medium` lands on now that the
app is on Material's own scale. This pair is a rejection that came back. The panel version
had no frame at all: the panel's own rounded outline said where the field was, so a second
box inside it would have been a box inside a box, and that argument holds *while the composer
floats*. A band has no outline, so the field needed its own — the report is "输入框形状改回
0.4.1 的样子，取代现在的悬浮样式" — and the frame is what makes the band read as a strip that
contains a place to write rather than as an area of the page.

What the panel bought, and what is given up with it, is worth naming, because it was a
deliberate design for a release. The panel floated: `PiShapes.panel` (28dp) on
`surfaceContainer`, inset 12dp on every side, with a 12dp **ambient-only** shadow
(`spotColor = Color.Transparent`, because a platform shadow's spot half is cast from above
and to one side and gave a full-width strip a dark band under one edge and none on the
other). That said "this is in front of the list" without a drawn line, and it let a message
scroll *past* the composer. What it could not say is where the field itself was, and the
reader's answer was that they wanted the field framed. A band in the column gives the same
separation by position — nothing overlaps, so nothing has to be told apart — and the fill
step does the rest.

The agent's **state** is deliberately not one of the chips: it is a fact rather than
a control, and a row of tappable chips with one inert chip at its head makes the eye
check all four every time. It is the app bar's subtitle, with the agent's mark in the
bar's action row beside it ([§7.3](07.3-transcript.md)). Nothing in the composer
carries the live coral either: coral means a turn is running, and a control the
reader is about to type into is not — the stop button, which *is* the "something is
running" control, is the `errorContainer` one and not a coral one.

What the state used to be drawn beside is worth keeping, because it is the reason a
mark is not simply placed next to the subtitle text: the coloured dot was an
`InlineTextContent` placeholder *inside* the text rather than a `Box` beside it,
since a `Row` of a `Box` and a `Text` gives the box no baseline, and at bodySmall the
5dp dot sat 3px low. The dot is gone with the flat header; a `Box` beside a line of
text is still the thing that measurement rules out.

The cache chip is measured against the **last finalized message**, not the session
total, because prompt caching is a property of one request: a ratio accumulated
over a session reports the average of a conversation that has changed shape several
times. It reads `0%` rather than hiding when nothing has been cached, because the
control people reach for would then disappear exactly when they go looking for it.

**The chip's fill is one step per palette, and the steps are not the same size, and
the design system's chip is what makes that the palette's question rather than the
call site's.** The row went 26dp-with-a-hairline to 34dp-with-`surfaceContainerHigh`
to `surfaceVariant`, and the last of those was still hard to see in the
light theme: on the composer's `#EDEEF2` bar, `#E4E9EF` is a 9/5/3-per-channel
difference, which at 34dp reads as a printed label rather than as a button.
`composerChipContainer()` took one more step — `surfaceContainer`, `#DCE3EB`,
another 8/6/4 down — and *no* step in the dark theme, where the same move would put
the chip at `#1A2028` on a `#171C23` bar and erase it, and the choice was made from
the fill's luminance, because the colour is the question being asked. That is a
luminance test at a call site to answer a question the palette should answer, and it
is what the design language means by a page not inventing a colour: the hand-rolled
chip and `composerChipContainer()` are both gone, and `PiSelectChip` is a 32dp pill
on `surfaceContainerHigh`, which against this well (`surfaceContainerLow`) is two
steps in **both** schemes — visible without asking the colour anything. Measured on
the emulator, the drawn pill is 84 px (32dp) inside its 48dp touch target.

The bar's three actions are ordered **compress, history, new**: left-to-right as
"do something to this conversation, look at the others, start another", and also the
order of how much each disturbs what is on screen — a compaction is applied in
place, the session list is a page away, and a new session replaces the transcript.
They sit after the agent's mark in the app bar's action row, which is where §7.3's
page frame puts them.

The field's own row holds three controls: the command list at the field's **left**
corner, and the `+` and the send (or stop) at its right, with a weighted spacer
between them so the layout does not depend on how wide the text is. Send and
stop are `FilledIconButton`s at a drawn 40dp — measured 105 px on the emulator —
with `PiShapes.pill` stated rather than inherited, because Material's own default
varies with the button's size in this release and "round" is the whole of what this
control is saying. The two states are the same shape on purpose: the reader is
looking for one button there, not two. The stop state is the one place in the
composer that is not the app's own voice — `errorContainer`, because stopping is the
action that cannot be taken back.

## Why the model switch is not a restart

Switching profile used to mean "make it active, then `scheduleRestart()`": a process
teardown, a `pi` start and a fresh handshake — seconds, and the whole transcript
re-read — to change one field that pi accepts over RPC through `set_model`. So the
switch is applied over RPC and the restart is only the fallback for when pi refuses,
which also means the running turn, the queue and the terminal's view of the session
survive it. Two details are load-bearing: the provider is sent as **pi's own provider
id** (`deepseek`, `pikit-custom`), because `set_model` matches `m.provider ===
command.provider` against the snapshot of models pi already knows — checked in pi
0.85.1's `rpc-mode.js`, which answers `Model not found: <provider>/<id>` for anything
else; the environment variable name is the same string for the built-in providers but
not the same concept, and sending `DEEPSEEK_API_KEY` is a lookup that always misses.
And the profile is committed to the store *first*, so the setting survives a later
restart even if the call is rejected — a rejection means this build does not offer
that model, and the fallback is a restart, which cannot be wrong.

## Why there is no segmented control in this app

A `SegmentedChoice` — one track, one marker sliding under the chosen segment —
lived in `Forms.kt` for a while, used for exactly one setting: the thinking level,
on the model page. It was removed rather than fixed for two reasons:

- **Seven Han labels do not fit a phone's width.** The control's own documentation
  said two to five options; the thinking levels are seven, so each segment got
  about 36dp and the touch targets were under the platform minimum.
- **It rendered as an empty box.** The control sized itself from its *first
  segment*: `BoxWithConstraints` wraps its content and the segments are sized from
  its marker, so seven options produced a ~150px rounded rectangle at the start of
  the row and every label ellipsised to nothing. Measured with `uiautomator`: not
  one text node inside the control's bounds, against seven rows in the sheet that
  replaced it.

The design system shipped a connected group of options (`PiConnectedGroup`, §13) for
a while — the guidance's own shape for a short set of alternatives shown side by side —
and it never sent the thinking level back to a segmented control: a segment that
does not render is a defect a better component fixes, but the first failure is about
how many options there are, and seven labels that do not fit a phone's width do not
fit it in a new component either. No page had a use for it in the end, and it was
deleted with its last call site.

**The composer's chip is the only control for it, and it opens a floating menu.** A
thinking level is a *range* from `off` to `max`, and the first answer to the chip was
a discrete slider (`ThinkingLevelSheet`, the same shape as the personalization page's
font-size sheet): the level under the thumb as the big name, its sentence underneath,
ticks on a track whose ends are pi's first and last ids, the drag a draft that
committed on release — applying mid-drag re-measured the composer's chip row under the
finger and made the drag stutter. The slider is gone, and the menu at the chip is the
shape every other choice in the app now wears (`PiMenuRow`, the anchored half): the
level ids as rows with a tick on the current one. The ids alone, no sentences under
them — seven two-line entries out of seven one-word choices was the report
`思考等级那里去掉每个等级下面的小字说明`; the sentences stay on
`thinkingLevelOptions` beside the ids, written once. What else the slider knew and the
menu keeps: the seven ids are the wire strings and are never translated, and a model
with one level gets a menu of one row where the slider drew a one-tick track.

`thinkingLevelOptions` is still the one place a level and its sentence are written: the
chip's menu builds its rows from it, and nothing else keeps a second list. The model
page used to carry the setting as a `PickerRow` at the top of **Settings → Model &
provider**, and that row is gone: one setting had grown two controls, and the composer's
chip is the one a conversation actually uses. The page lists profiles, and the level
appears on it only as a *fact* — the level each model would run at is on that model's own
row, clamped by `clampThinkingLevel` against the levels pi last reported for it — which is
what makes one helper enough for a chip, a menu and a row.

## Sheets are drawn in the page's own window, not in a second one

Every modal in the app — the pickers, the conversation's numbers, the command list,
the file preview, the terminal's session switcher — used to be a Material3
`ModalBottomSheet`, which is not a view in the page but a `ComponentDialog`
(`ModalBottomSheetDialogWrapper`) laid over the activity. Neither of the two things
wrong with it could be fixed from the call sites, because a dialog window is defined
to take window focus.

**It closed the keyboard.** A focusable window appearing over a focused text field
takes the IME's connection with it. Measured: `mImeWindowVis=3 mInputShown=true`
before tapping a composer chip, `mImeWindowVis=0 mInputShown=false` 250 ms after.
Three animations then ran at once and disagreed — the keyboard leaving, the page
re-measuring as the IME's inset collapsed (the composer's chips moved 610 px, from
y=1153 to y=1763), and the sheet travelling in against an inset that was still
shrinking under it, so it arrived somewhere other than where it settled.

**It swallowed input after closing.** The window outlives the state that asked for
it. Counting `pi.kit.mob` windows with `dumpsys window` after a dismiss-then-tap
pair at increasing gaps: one window while open, and the tap after the dismissal was
lost at gaps of 150, 250 and 500 ms and only landed at 800 ms. Roughly 0.6–0.9 s of
dead screen, per sheet, everywhere a sheet was used.

A sheet is now a `Box` in the page's own window (`ui/components/Sheets.kt`), drawn
by the root above the tab strip. There is no second window, so there is nothing to
take focus, nothing to re-measure and nothing to outlive the state. Two
consequences are deliberate:

- **The keyboard stays up**, and the panel is lifted above it by
  `WindowInsets.ime`, so a picker opened mid-sentence appears over the keyboard
  rather than replacing it. The page underneath does not move at all.
- **A leaving sheet takes no input.** The panel's `draggable` and every row's
  `clickable` are installed only while the host says the sheet is open, so once it
  has been dismissed a tap reaches whatever is underneath — the same measurement
  now reads: lost at a 0.00–0.10 s gap, landed from 0.15 s on, which is the 250 ms
  of its own exit animation and nothing more. A pointer modifier that is merely
  *disabled* is not enough: it still claims the hit test, so a row on its way off
  screen would eat the tap instead of letting it through.

`SheetHost` holds one sheet — modal means never two — and keeps the last one for the
length of the exit, which is what the dialog's window used to do for free. The state
lives above the root's `key(language)` block: switching the language rebuilds every
screen under it, and the sheet it was switched *from* still has to finish sliding
out. A sheet's body is a composable rather than a row spec because the file preview
is not a list, and a body that must follow live data reads it inside the lambda,
since the lambda is built once, when the sheet opens. Only the *content* of a sheet
is drawn by the sheet: the panel, its shape, the grabber, the scrim, the back gesture
and the drag all belong to the layer, so the picker and the command list cannot
drift apart. The *bodies* are the design system's now — `PiSheetTitle`,
`PiSheetList`, `PiSheetRow`, `PiSheetActions` (§13) — so every sheet in the app is drawn
from the same parts and none of them can drift from another either.

**A modifier's `graphicsLayer` alpha reaches only the modifiers after it in the chain, and
the scrim is where getting that wrong ships as a black page.** The catcher was
`background(scrim).graphicsLayer { alpha = … }`: a modifier wraps the ones after it, so
the fill painted *outside* the layer at full strength whatever the alpha said, and every
menu and sheet opened over an opaque black page in both themes
(`点击按钮弹出悬浮菜单后，后面的内容全部黑屏了`). The order is
`graphicsLayer { alpha = … }.background(scrim)`. The layer's older version of the same
code was fine for a reason that is easy to lose: the colour itself carried the alpha
(`scrim.copy(alpha = …)`), so there was no layer for the order to be wrong in. The rule
generalises — an alpha that governs a paint belongs in a layer ahead of that paint, whose
lambda is read at draw time.

**`Sheet`'s `content` is the last constructor parameter, and that is load-bearing.** Every
call site passes it as a trailing lambda, so a parameter added after it breaks all of them
at once; the optionals (`anchor`, `alignEnd`, `fullBleedBottom`, `preferAbove`) come first.

**The conversation list's row menu was the last `DropdownMenu` in the app.** `Popup`
emits a zero-size layout node into its caller's layout, and the row was a `Row` with
`Arrangement.spacedBy(4.dp)` — which does not skip zero-size children — so merely
opening the menu slid the ⋮ button 4 dp to the left and narrowed the title column by
the same amount. And a `DropdownMenu` is anchored to the composable that contains
it, so it opened under the *row's* left edge rather than under the button, over the
title it belonged to. It became a sheet body first, and it is a *floating menu* now —
which is the anchored half of this same layer, and the answer to what a sheet does
with a question as small as one row's three actions.

## The anchored half: a menu at the thing it is about

A `Sheet` that carries an anchor (`Menus.kt`) is drawn by the same layer in the same
window — one modal, one back handler, the same tap gates on the rows — and differs in
where its panel goes and what it costs: the panel is placed at the anchor's
window-relative bounds (below the trigger, flipped above it when the space below is
short, clamped inside the window; start-aligned to the trigger, or end-aligned when
the caller asks — a row's action menu hangs off the trailing edge where a row's
actions live — or when a start-aligned panel would run past the window's end), the
scrim does not dim, and there is no grabber and no drag. A trigger reports its bounds
with `menuAnchor` — an `onGloballyPositioned` read that changes nothing about its
layout, which is the property the `Popup` could never offer. The rows are
`PiMenuRow`: 44dp, as wide as the widest row, a tick at the end, the same tap gate a
sheet row carries.

**The composer's four menus always open above their trigger.** The chips and buttons
that ask for a menu sit above the field the reader is typing into, and a panel that
drops below them lands on that field — the report "输入框上方的按钮点击出的悬浮菜单应该
始终从上面出现而不是从下面出现，否则会遮挡输入框". `Sheet.preferAbove` is the caller
overriding the auto rule: above wins whenever the space above can hold the panel, and
the auto rule (below when it fits and above does not, otherwise the taller side)
answers only when it cannot. The window clamp still bounds a panel taller than either
side.

**The shadow rides the fade.** The first version hung the menu's shadow on the
`Surface`'s `shadowElevation`, and the platform composites an elevation shadow
*outside* the `graphicsLayer` alpha that fades the panel in — so the panel arrived
over 125 ms while its shadow was at full strength on the first frame, which is the
report "悬浮菜单出现时后面的阴影过于生硬，是突然出现的那种". The elevation is now set
inside the same `graphicsLayer` lambda that reads `progress`, scaled by it: no
shadow at 0, the menu's full shadow at 1, on the one clock.

**The panel is placed in one measure pass, and it answers at dropdown speed.** Two
reports — "点击到出现都不够迅速且不流畅丝滑" — had one cause each. *Not smooth*: the
first version measured the panel after composing it, so the opening frame was laid out
from a zero size and corrected itself the next frame — a visible jump mid-fade, on
every menu that flips above its trigger (all of the composer's chips). The panel is a
`SubcomposeLayout` now: content composed, measured and placed inside one layout pass,
so the first frame is the frame it stays on. *Not rapid*: the layer's one 250 ms clock
is the page transition's, and a dropdown that takes a quarter-second to appear at the
control that was tapped reads as a pause — an anchored menu runs on 125 ms, the
platform `DropdownMenu`'s own fade-and-scale. Both halves of the animation read
`SheetHost.progress` inside `graphicsLayer`, at draw time; reading it in composition
rebuilt the whole modal layer on every frame of every entrance and exit, which was the
rest of the stutter. The scale grows out of the corner that faces the trigger — the
top edge when the menu drops below it, the bottom edge when it flips above, and
horizontally the edge the panel is *aligned* by — so a menu whose panel hangs to the
left of its trigger (the composer's `+`, whose right edge is the screen's) still
appears from under the button instead of coming in from the screen's other side
(`按钮在右边，动画效果却是菜单从左边弹出来的`). The sheet's translation would carry a
menu up from the window's edge, straight through the control it opened from.

The rule for which surface is which: a *choice the panel hangs off* is a menu, a
*report or a form* is a sheet. In practice:

- **the composer's thinking-level and model chips** open menus at the chip — the two
  choices a sentence is sent with, read once and tapped once. The `/model` command
  row opens the same model menu at the same chip, which is why that one anchor is
  owned by `ChatScreen` rather than by the chip;
- **the composer's ⌘ and + buttons** open menus at the button — the command list
  (each row runs on the tap; the `!` row opens the shell-line *sheet*, because a
  line to type needs a field) and the attach choices (camera, image, file). Both
  were sheets and both are short menus of things to *do*;
- **the conversation list's row actions** (hold a row) open a menu on the row —
  rename, pin, delete;
- **the terminal's session switcher** opens a menu at its toolbar button — the
  question is "which terminal", asked over the terminal.

Everything else stays a docked sheet: the conversation's numbers is a report to
read, and the forms (the AGENTS.md editor, the model numbers, the avatar picker)
need the bottom edge for their buttons. Settings' language row is a **dialog** —
the one selection the reader asked to be a 弹窗 (`语言选择改为弹窗选择的形式`), and
the shape fits: a question, its three answers under it, a confirm and a cancel.

## A list scrolls to the panel's edge; the inset is inside the scroll

A sheet body used to be padded by the navigation bar's height at the panel level, and
that padding is what stood between a scrolling list's text and the window's bottom
edge: the text stopped at the padding, the padding showed the panel's own fill, and a
scrolling list looked like it had a bar of white under it for as long as it scrolled
(`滑动时文字不会直接触底而是会被底下一个白色条块遮挡`). The same band showed on the
fetched-model list and every other list on the layer, because the inset was applied
once per panel, above whatever scrolled.

A body that scrolls now declares it (`Sheet.fullBleedBottom`), the layer hands it the
inset through `LocalSheetBottomInset` instead of padding the panel, and `PiSheetList`
carries it as the list's own bottom content padding — the inset is then *part of the
scroll*: text travels the full height of the panel, and the last item still clears the
gesture bar when the list is scrolled to its end. A body with something after its list
(a footnote, an action row, a form's buttons) still owes the inset itself, below that
tail, which is what `PickerBody`'s and this file's forms' own paddings do.
